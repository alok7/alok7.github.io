1. Executive Verdict

Thesis type: working-capital-intensive industrial business with a conditional cash-conversion turnaround and unproven solar diversification, not an established compounder. Hold — unresolved. Shivalic Power Control is an established electrical-panel manufacturer moving into solar EPC and project subsidiaries, not yet an evidence-backed high-return compounder. Historical core revenue expansion is real and engineered/OEM-qualified products offer useful customer access. The decisive weakness is that working-capital investment has grown much faster than operating profit: three successive years of negative parent CFO and low incremental capital returns contradict a cash-generative scaling thesis. The ₹63.15 NSE close implies about 12.94× FY26 owner-attributable group earnings, not the superficially cheaper multiple using all group profit. It is not enough to establish an attractive entry. Conflicting cash/FD and guarantee disclosures, a missing current funding bridge and unvalued new subsidiary commitments leave BQS and IAS NS. GQS is computable but capped at 5.0 for weak cash realization and incremental returns, and is excluded from quality rankings while G2 remains unresolved. A corrected liquidity/claims bridge, sustained collections and demonstrated owner-level returns from both core and subsidiaries would change the assessment.

4. Business Overview

What the company actually sells

The historical core is engineered-to-order low-tension and high-tension electrical panels: power-control centres, motor-control centres, intelligent MCCs, DG synchronisation, APFC, VFD, distribution boards and bus ducts. It designs, fabricates, powder-coats, assembles, wires and tests at its owned Faridabad facility. It buys much of the expensive switchgear and electrical content rather than inventing or manufacturing all of it. The RHP lists switchgears, CRCA sheet, aluminium/copper bars, wire and paint among inputs, and direct billing with switchgear OEMs. LT panels were 93.93% and HT 6.07% of revenue in the nine months to December 2023, versus 97.88%/2.12% in FY23. The RHP's table calls the stub '2023-24'; its total matches the nine-month revenue, not the full FY24 revenue. Do not present the 93.93% as audited full-year FY24 mix. S1 pp 105-109,120

Three routes to market in the December 2024 investor deck are partner-branded panels, work routed by EPC contractors, and Shivalic-branded panels. The company says technical partners provide warranties on the first category whereas Shivalic carries warranty responsibility on its own brand. It presents 1,000-plus installations, 500-plus clients, 20-plus sectors, and a 50% customer-retention rate. These are management operating claims, not independently audited market-share or repeat-revenue measures. S5 PDF6,16-17

The core economic engine is gross profit on custom panels less engineering, selling and assembly costs, not recurring software/service subscriptions. The RHP specifically says no after-sales services; the later deck proposes an AMC approach. No material audited AMC revenue or recurring-services base was found by cutoff, so recurring revenue is ND rather than assumed. S1 p 122; S5 PDF30

From late FY26, the business has broadened into solar EPC, power infrastructure, EHV and proposed energy storage. Consolidation changes comparability: the FY26 group includes two acquired companies only from their acquisition dates, while FY25 comparatives are SPCL alone. Core standalone performance must remain visible. S4 NSE PDF259

5. Moat and Industry Position

Advantage and its boundaries

The real advantages evidenced are execution capability, technical approvals, integrated fabrication/test processes, reference projects and relationships. The RHP states licensed/franchise or EcoXpert relationships with L&T Electrical & Automation, Siemens, Schneider Electric and TDK for type-tested panels. The deck shows product-specific IEC/IS testing and fully bolted modular design, allowing expansion and configurability. Those attributes can help win specification-sensitive industrial and data-centre projects, and direct OEM buying can lower purchase prices. S1 pp 108-109,120-121; S5 PDF18-21

This does not establish a proprietary or exclusive moat. The RHP itself warns that OEM forward integration, cancellation or non-renewal could interrupt sales. Some supplier tie-ups require annual renewal; the company otherwise has no long-term supply agreements. Customer business is purchase-order based, without long-term customer contracts. Neither a protected patent franchise, exclusive territory, quantified switching costs, persistent pricing premium nor independent market share has been demonstrated. Treat homepage 'only company' or 'exclusive' language as unverified marketing, not evidence of monopoly. S1 pp 29-32

Direct OEM sourcing is not a free margin gain: the company explicitly links the improved prices/discounts obtained since FY23 to earlier payment than dealers, lower payable days and greater funding requirements. Its move to large corporate orders similarly trades potential margin/scale benefits for longer receivables and on-floor order cycles. Thus the relevant moat test is cash return on the additional capital, not merely EBITDA percentage. S1 p 75

Management's FY26 MD&A still lists price competition from unorganised players, raw-material volatility, long government-linked payment cycles and dependence on a few large customers/EPC contractors. These are issuer counterevidence against a strong-pricing-power narrative. S4 NSE PDF151-152

Comparable method and numerical snapshot

Operating EBIT = profit before exceptional items and tax + finance cost - other income. Operating EBITDA = that EBIT + depreciation/amortisation. Divide both by revenue from operations, not total income. These are analyst calculations, not management-adjusted EBITDA. They retain all items recorded within operating expenses, including litigation costs. This removes the most obvious inconsistent treatment of interest/other income but does not homogenise product mix, leasing, accounting standards or expenses.

Cash and capital definitions

- CFO is the reported net operating cash flow after tax, before financing interest where separately classified. CFO/EBITDA is a directional conversion measure, not a fully comparable cash-tax/interest-adjusted return.

- Capex is gross cash spent on PPE/intangibles/CWIP and explicit capital-advance lines, not the entire investing cash flow. Marine's line includes investment property and movements in capital creditors/advances; Vivid includes PPE, intangibles and CWIP but not its separately described loans/advances; Saakshi includes fixed-asset purchases/CWIP plus capital advances; RMC uses its PPE/CWIP purchase line. These differences are material and disclosed, not silently netted.

- Gross borrowings sums current and non-current borrowings. Separately reported lease liabilities are excluded. Reported cash equivalents and other bank balances are separate: bank deposits cannot all be treated as freely available net-debt offsets.

- Simple operating working capital = receivables + inventory - trade payables. It intentionally excludes contract assets, advances, other operating assets/liabilities and taxes. Closing receivable days = year-end receivables/revenue × 365, a snapshot proxy, not collection DSO based on average balances.

- Do not rank reported ROCE: definitions differ and some omit an explicit averaging convention.

Shivalic Power Control: monthly model methodology

Interpretation comes before the numbers

This is a standalone-core scenario and funding model, not a consolidated price target. Every ordinary common-equity IRR and total-company price is NS. A funded path can report the panel business's equity contribution per current or conditionally diluted share; it cannot tell us the value of Somaya Solar, Prima Solar, Religreen, Seedlab, their financing, or the recoverability of the parent's loans. Missing subsidiary value is not assigned zero. A core-only illustrative IRR is explicitly a sufficiency diagnostic: what the modeled core contribution alone would return against the entire quoted entry price. It is not a prediction of the shareholder's total return.

The valuation price is the verified NSE Emerge close of ₹63.15 on 19 October 2026. The model uses 24,115,754 issued shares, not the previous year's weighted-average EPS denominator. All monetary amounts in the model are ₹ crore, except per-share prices and explicitly identified rupee amounts. Decimal arithmetic uses 40-digit precision; JSON stores unrounded decimal strings.

Date and entity boundaries

The source operating balance sheet is standalone 31 March 2026. Forecast months are labelled November 2026 onward: 60 months for the five-year model and 120 months for the ten-year model, each followed by 12 explicit terminal-transition months. There is no verified April–October operating, working-capital, cash or debt bridge. These are therefore conditional paths seeded with an old balance sheet, not reconstructed actual monthly accounts or verified October liquidity.

The ₹1.50 crore Prima loan disclosed after March and ₹0.0143 crore Seedlab consideration are separately charged in the first model month as a transparent pro forma funding stress. The first-month date is not asserted to be their actual payment date. The balance sheet and those later commitments are not silently mixed and presented as a current audited opening. A first-month shortfall is a model funding gap, not evidence of a default. The annual audit's reported absence of default is not overridden by the model.

Consolidated revenue, cash, inventory and debt are not inserted into standalone cash generation. The first FY2026 consolidated period includes acquisition-date results; it is not a full-year organic operating history for the subsidiaries. Group PAT of ₹13.1182 crore includes approximately ₹1.3496 crore of NCI profit. Owner PAT and EPS, rather than total group PAT divided by parent shares, are the applicable equity-earnings cross-check.

Physical panel bridge and capex

The RHP disclosed 10,000 verticals of annual installed capacity on three shifts, with FY2023 production of 3,900. That is historical capacity, not a current output certificate. FY2023 revenue of ₹82.1568 crore divided by 3,900 gives a historical revenue-per-vertical proxy. Three years of explicitly assumed 3% price/mix escalation produce ₹0.02301921887 crore per panel-equivalent and approximately 6,240.84 panel-equivalents for the FY2026 standalone revenue anchor.

These are analytical equivalents, not reported FY2026 physical units. Standalone EPC and product mix may make the proxy inaccurate. The model exposes that limitation rather than silently applying a revenue CAGR unrelated to physical production. Revenue equals delivered panel-equivalents times assumed price. Delivery is capped by commissioned capacity. Unfulfilled demand and terminal lost units are retained.

Conditional expansion costs ₹0.0035 crore per unit of annual vertical capacity, has a six-month commissioning lag and seeks 20% capacity headroom. This is an analyst capital-intensity assumption, with a 50% cost-inflation case and a 12-month lag case. Source-bounded paths cannot assume additional installed capacity.

The March 2026 remaining IPO core-capex earmark is ₹5.7146 crore: ₹1.08 crore machinery and ₹4.6346 crore assembly work. It is spent over six scenario months and commissioned after the same lag. This remaining amount might have been spent before the research cutoff; completion is not verified. No quantified capacity uplift is invented for this IPO spending. Conditional incremental capacity is modeled and paid for separately. That is intentionally conservative but may overstate future required expenditure if the IPO work already created usable capacity; the uncertainty is not hidden.

Maintenance capex is 2% of sales in the reference path. Terminal maintenance must also cover the modeled replacement allowance, and terminal replacement capex cannot be less than D&A. Payments enter the cash flow when made, assets enter PPE only on commissioning, and D&A follows commissioned assets. Paid pipeline balances are not counted as freely available cash.

Monthly operating cash mechanics

Opening receivables are collected over an explicit number of months, with recovery haircuts only in identified stresses. New credit sales enter a dated collection queue. Customer advances are both received and applied separately; the same sale cannot be collected twice. Opening supplier balances have their own payment queue. Material purchases equal material use plus the required inventory build, subject to no negative purchases. Supplier payments follow the selected payment lag.

The reference operating assumptions are 7% annual panel-equivalent volume growth, 2% price growth, 11% operating EBITDA margin, 210 inventory days, four-month new-receivable collection lag and one-month new-purchase supplier lag. Source-bounded base inventory is 240 days, close to the historical year-end proxy of 243 days. The improvement to 210 days is a conditional normalization, not established cash conversion. There is also a 245-day case and a six-month collections case.

The material ratio comes from FY2026 standalone disclosures. Other operating costs are split between 55% fixed and 45% sales-sensitive components. Thus customer loss affects utilization, operating leverage and cash generation, not merely the terminal multiple. Cash tax is 26% of positive post-interest profit; no loss tax asset is monetized. Operating FCFF removes financing and uses tax on positive operating EBIT. Other income, deposit interest and uncollected related-loan interest are not treated as recurring operating cash generation.

Deposits, facilities and maturity gates

Deposit availability is unresolved. Source-bounded cases release no deposit and invent no new borrowing headroom. The conditional reference releases all ₹16.2297 crore, renews the existing cash-credit balance and permits up to ₹25 crore of hypothetical new debt, with a ₹50 crore cumulative new-money limit. The reference may not actually draw that capacity; realized draws are shown. The no-new-debt case tests whether the permission is necessary. Conditional facility existence or renewal is not an actual sanction or an authorisation.

Cash-credit interest is assumed at 10.5%; a demand-call stress repays its entire outstanding balance in month 12. That month is a stress convention, not a discovered expiry date. The old prospectus's ₹30 crore WCDL limit and 90-day tenor are historical context only and do not establish current available headroom.

Seven term facilities use their printed opening balances, rates, EMIs and stated maturity dates. Each month's principal is the lesser of balance and EMI less monthly interest. Any residual is paid at the stated maturity. The HDFC MSME EMI is ND, so the model retains interest and pays a bullet at its July 2027 maturity as a stress convention, not a verified contractual amortization schedule. The March-to-November missing bridge also affects these stale opening loans. A ₹0.001 lakh difference between summed facility balances and reported term debt is explicitly reconciled in the final facility, not silently discarded. A separate immediate-call stress is retained.

New debt bears 13.5% interest and 1.5% draw fees. Current-month interest is charged on the actual draw as well as opening debt. The draw calculation solves for the cash requirement including that interest and the available tax shield; it does not fund a deficit and then omit the financing cost that caused a new deficit. New-money limits, three-year maturities, renewals and fees are explicit. No new draw or equity rescue is allowed in the terminal transition. Any allowed pre-terminal refinancing is distinctly conditional.

The minimum operating reserve is the greater of ₹0.50 crore and 15% of a month's material and cash operating cost. A path stops at its first unmet reserve or liquidity obligation. Its failure row, negative or positive remaining cash and exact shortfall remain in the canonical archive. No ordinary investor return is emitted for failed paths.

Somaya's 51.64% ownership and Prima's 51.22% reported actual allotment are distinct from 100% group assets and profits. Prima's 51% approval wording conflicts with the 10,500-of-20,500 actual allotment and AOC percentage; the discrepancy remains visible. Seedlab is a separately disclosed 51% acquisition. The ₹6.5500782 crore historical acquisition cost is neither added to core value as if a verified market price nor treated as the subsidiaries' future cash flow.

The Prima loan is a separate parent financial claim. ₹1.50 crore is disclosed as already advanced after March; the ₹20 crore maximum is not assumed paid. The full-limit stress substitutes ₹20 crore total for the ₹1.50 crore case, so it does not add the first advance twice. The specific approval states 9% interest, but collection and principal-repayment timing are not established. The model assumes no receipt without a separate condition and leaves recoverable loan value NS. That is a cash-flow permission boundary, not a conclusion that the loan is worthless.

The ₹100 crore related-party transaction authorizations for Somaya and Prima are limits, not revenues, completed payments or separate lending facilities. They are not included as operating assets or funding.

Disclosed guarantees are contingent, not ordinary funded debt. The ₹8.632 crore HDFC grid-connection BG and ₹20.9115 crore older standalone guarantee disclosure each have their own called-guarantee stress. Their overlap is not resolved, so they are not summed as distinct debt or paid capex. A call reduces cash only in its selected scenario and is not also subtracted as outstanding debt in the terminal bridge.

The approved ESOP pool is 300,000 options, not 600,000 and not already issued capital. Approval alone supplies no cash. The full-pool exercise sensitivity adds 300,000 shares and ₹0.30 crore cash at the ₹10 strike in month 48. Vesting and grants are not assumed to have occurred in the source-bounded scenarios. This is an explicit dilution stress, not a forecast issuance schedule.

Earned terminal value and no cash/debt double count

The engine runs another full year after each investment horizon. Terminal growth is normally 4%; WACC is 16%. A 13–17% range is the reference cost-of-capital discussion, while the 19% grid point is an adverse-rate stress. The WACC/g grid tests 13%, 15%, 17%, 19% against 2%, 4%, 6%. Equity-hurdle tests are separate at 12%, 15% and 18%.

Terminal operating NOPAT is earned from this extra year, not mechanically extrapolated from reported PAT. Replacement capex is at least D&A. Growth reinvestment is the larger of physical growth capex plus positive operating-working-capital growth and NOPAT × terminal growth / terminal ROIC. Terminal ROIC is a conditional 14% recovery assumption, not achieved historical performance; 8% and 10% cases test weaker reinvestment economics. Terminal FCFF equals NOPAT plus D&A less replacement and growth reinvestment. Terminal EV is FCFF divided by WACC less growth, floored at zero only for the limited-liability core claim calculation.

The model computes the largest cash requirement over every transition month, including any additional replacement and reinvestment not already paid in the monthly cash schedule. A terminal value is suppressed if that requirement is not funded or if the extra year cannot meet demand with physical capacity. No exit distribution can leave the transition below the reserve.

Funding reserves and valuation operating reserves are different. Cash earmarked for debt principal, interest net of tax shield, or other separately deducted senior claims is added back when calculating the operating reserve. The equity bridge then adds cash above that operating reserve and subtracts debt and remaining senior claims exactly once. It does not deduct debt-service cash from assets and the same debt again from equity. Restricted or uncertain deposits are not automatically added. No modeled dividends are distributed during the horizon, so retained cash is not counted twice as both dividends and terminal cash.

An EBITDA multiple cross-check uses 8× in the reference, with 6× normalization. These are analytical multiples, not verified peer trading medians. The explicit operating FCFF DCF and terminal share of that DCF are separately retained; terminal dependence can exceed 100% when the explicit investment period consumes cash.

Scenario families, reverse expectations and targets

Bear, base and bull preserve source-bounded financing gates and differ in customer loss, volume, pricing, margins, collections, inventory and capex timing. They are not assigned probabilities or averaged into an expected return.

Conditional cases separately test FD release, new-debt permission, CC renewal, new-debt renewal, inventory normalization, collections, commissioning delays, capex inflation, Prima funding, called guarantees, option exercise, IPO-only deposit release, later subsidiary outflows, maturity acceleration, lower exit multiples and terminal reinvestment returns. Both five- and ten-year horizons are run.

The growth/margin grid uses volume growth of 0%, 5%, 10%, 15% and 20% and EBITDA margins of 8%, 11% and 14%. Reverse expectations use a saved 0–40% volume-growth grid in two-percentage-point steps. A wider target envelope tests 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, 60% growth and 11%, 14%, 17% margins, with larger but entirely hypothetical capacity and financing limits. The full tested grid, including failed funding paths, is retained. A selected crossing is only the least tested grid point; it is not an exact continuous threshold or an achievable forecast.

Targets include 12%, 15%, 18% annual hurdles and 3×, 5×, 10× net wealth over five and ten years. They ask whether the core contribution alone would be sufficient. They do not silently declare the subsidiaries worthless or value their upside. For funded reference paths, the model also calculates the additional owner-attributable non-core exit equity required to meet each hurdle. A negative residual means the modeled core contribution alone is sufficient under those conditions, not that a negative subsidiary valuation is predicted.
