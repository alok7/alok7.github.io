1. Executive Verdict

Shri Techtex is a polypropylene nonwoven, technical-fabric and jobwork business with real operating assets, but the investable thesis is a cash-recovery/value case rather than an evidence-backed compounder. BQS 4.4 and GQS 4.8; IAS NS. FY2026 sales grew only 1.76% while reported PAT grew 31.46%; after removing other income and the disclosed power subsidy, operating EBIT was almost unchanged. Cash became tied up in raw materials, group advances and an uncommissioned solar project. The ₹61.90 close looks inexpensive on reported earnings, but that comparison omits subsidy dependence, financial-asset recovery and extremely thin execution capacity. A conservative historical-balance base can fund itself and produces a 14.06% five-year IRR, below the 15% hurdle; changing only the undisclosed related-party repayment maturity can make it unfinanceable. Hold — unresolved current funding, not a finding of fraud or observed default. Obtain the Aurum Nets agreement/current cash bridge and evidence of inventory conversion before upgrading the investment assessment. F05, F07, B02

4. Business Overview

The company makes polypropylene nonwoven fabric at Dholka and supplies jobwork and technical-fabric products. Its disclosed nonwoven capacity is 3,600 tonnes annually. New hot-melt coating/lamination and polypropylene multifilament lines were reported operational in FY2025, but line-specific revenue and utilization were not found. Applications include agriculture, disposable/medical uses, furnishing and industrial fabrics; these are applications, not proof of approvals or share gains.

FY2026 product revenue ₹69.7144 crore and services ₹14.4138 crore produced total ₹84.1282 crore. Jobwork ₹14.3638 crore is almost the entire service line. Jobwork for two related parties, Shri Jagdamba Polymers and Shakti Polyweave, totals ₹13.7795 crore: 95.93% of jobwork, but only 16.38% of total revenue. Conflating those denominators would exaggerate overall concentration. IPO-era FY2023 top-five customers accounted for 94.08% and top three 84.40%; current whole-business customer concentration remains ND. No enforceable backlog or customer-volume commitment is assumed. 5. Moat / Industry Position

An established plant, flexible specifications and relationships create useful commercial positioning, but the evidence does not establish durable pricing power, patents, high switching costs or a protected cost advantage. Related-party demand is commercially real but cannot independently validate a moat. Polypropylene prices, domestic competition and customer mix can alter economics. The strongest contrary evidence is that the company remained profitable through a large export-to-domestic shift and brought new lines into operation. That supports a functioning business, not an exceptional competitive barrier.

7. Growth Quality & Reinvestment Runway

GQS 4.8 reflects positive post-demerger expansion but only mixed reinvestment and weak cash realization. REV=5 does not reward profit growth, TAM, new capacity or low valuation. DUR=5 recognizes real applications and installed lines while withholding a high runway score absent line utilization/customer evidence. CAP=5 recognizes a roughly 17.01% FY2023–26 incremental proxy, but no-power-subsidy FY2024–26 increment is far weaker; CASH=4 is below adequate because even classification-adjusted cumulative conversion is only about 0.70.

Bottom-up physical output cannot be reconstructed from current tonnage disclosures: the 3,600-tonne nonwoven plant, 3,360-tonne lamination line and 1,200-tonne yarn line are different products and cannot simply be added and multiplied by an invented selling price. The model instead uses an explicitly analytical ₹125 crore monetary ceiling, sensitivity ₹100–150 crore, and expansion cash equal to 45% of incremental capacity revenue, with 18-month lead time. This is a capacity-and-funding test, not management guidance or a verified unit model.

Base demand growth is 8%, below the 13.9% post-demerger historical CAGR but above latest 1.76%. Operating EBITDA anchor 24% is below FY2026 ex-power-subsidy 25.11%; modest operating leverage is modeled and realized margins shown separately. Receivable/inventory days transition over 24 months, not instant liquidation. Replacement spending is not set equal to depreciation. Source-bounded main cases assume no new debt, equity or loan recovery. The decisive runway constraint is whether products turn inventory and invested capital into independent repeat cash earnings, rather than whether a broad technical-textile market exists. 8. Management, Governance & Execution

The audit is unmodified, no severe minority abuse was established, and the predecessor auditor's resignation was attributed to workload rather than blocked information. Those facts matter, but do not prove recoverability or excellent stewardship. The MD also became CFO in July 2025; concentration of responsibilities is a monitoring point, not an automatic violation. MD remuneration ₹0.6024 crore is about 3.12% of FY2026 PAT and is not the principal extraction concern.

CARO identifies ₹12.2508 crore loans/advances, ₹9.0029 crore to related parties, with no stipulated repayment or interest schedules. “Not overdue” follows from no repayment demand, not demonstrated performance against due dates. Harmony Finvest partnership investment is ₹13.1252 crore. The main model does not use these assets as cash or capitalize their interest income into operating EBITDA. Aurum Nets ₹6.25 crore is loan received in the RPT transaction table, not an extra receivable merely because a balance table lacks signs. Its mapping into customer advances is analytical and requires issuer clarification. AOC-2 arm's-length assertion and auditor non-prejudicial conclusion are material contrary evidence; no diversion allegation is inferred. B02, F05

11. Bear Case & Permanent-Loss Path

The most important variable is cash available before obligations become payable, not reported EPS. A domestic demand disappointment lowers utilization and margin; elevated raw-polypropylene inventory then takes longer to sell or requires discounting; group advances cannot be demanded on a known schedule; an early Aurum repayment or guarantee call creates a cash gap. New debt or discounted shares may fill it, but that changes per-share economics. The worst credible outcome is a profitable-looking balance sheet whose assets cannot be converted into cash on creditors' timetable.

The strongest evidence against a purely negative interpretation is that bank debt fell materially, net worth is substantial, receivables declined, the base can fund itself without loan recovery, and new manufacturing lines were reported commissioned. Therefore this is not an asserted default, fraud or inevitable recapitalization. Conversely, cheap reported P/E does not disprove illiquidity or recoverability risks. A stock-price decline alone does not cure an unknown short-term maturity. Documented repayment terms, actual bank balances, inventory aging/sales realization and cash collections would materially change the conclusion. F05, F07, B02
