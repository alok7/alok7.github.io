1. Executive Verdict

Insufficient evidence for an investment conclusion; an engineering-manufacturing expansion thesis, not an evidence-backed compounder designation. Shri Balaji Valve Components makes precision valve components and assemblies for industrial customers. Its useful manufacturing and qualification capabilities coexist with customer bargaining power, working-capital intensity and a substantial reinvestment requirement. Reported FY26 revenue is ₹96.8071 crore, but historical perimeter changes and unquantified price/mix effects prevent treating headline growth as verified organic demand growth. The ₹134 secondary 19 October close implies ₹109.344 crore equity value; neither a low-looking earnings multiple nor a conditional model solves the financial-control, cash-access and execution questions. Resolve the specific bank-return/accounting reconciliations, verify current financing and primary trading evidence, and demonstrate growth with cash after reinvestment before revisiting eligibility. This is an evidence-limited research assessment, not a finding of fraud or an instruction for an existing holder to sell. B01; F01; MKT_IIFL; MKT_EP

4. Business Overview

Business

Shri Balaji Valve Components Limited is a component manufacturer, not a branded finished-valve producer. It makes ready-to-assemble balls, stems, bonnets, bodies, trunnions, flanges, seats and related precision-machined parts to customer drawings. Valve OEMs incorporate these parts into products used mainly in oil and gas, with exposure to power, petrochemicals, pharmaceuticals and other industries. Management explicitly said defence exposure was small and that it cannot always identify the final end industry from the component. Do not describe this as an established defence or railway compounder: railway selling was only an exploration announced in the FY26 chairman's message. B01, pp. 8–14, 18, 40; B02, 11:22–12:09 and 26:53–27:47

The process starts with the customer's request for quotation, feasibility and costing, followed by a purchase order, material procurement, forging/cutting and heat treatment, machining, inspection and dispatch. Specifications, batch traceability and customer/third-party inspections matter because an incorrectly machined component can delay assembly or compromise valve operation. Revenue depends on winning successive qualified orders, not on an annuity license or disclosed take-or-pay contract. B01, pp. 9–11; B03, risk factors and business section

There are three Pune/Chakan facilities. Plants I and III perform machining; Plant II performs forging and heat treatment. The FY26 report describes 130-plus machines, including 25 CNCs, 16 VMCs and eight HMCs, three forging hammers and two heat-treatment furnaces. The new plant became operational in November 2025. These are issuer operational disclosures, not independently inspected assets. The advantages of integration are shorter supplier coordination, tighter traceability and ability to provide a larger component basket. The costs are plant investment, skilled operators, working capital and execution complexity. B01, pp. 8, 13–14

FY26 geographical revenue was ₹70.7400 crore domestic and ₹26.0671 crore export, totaling ₹96.8071 crore. Domestic business supplied ₹12.8970 crore, or 81.6%, of the ₹15.8000 crore revenue addition; exports supplied ₹2.9030 crore, or 18.4%. Export share fell from 28.60% to 26.93% despite export growth. Fourteen export countries do not demonstrate fourteen equally diversified revenue pools; country and customer profitability are not disclosed. The note on FOB foreign earnings gives ₹25.7500593 crore, not ₹26.0671 crore: retain the distinction rather than silently merging the two. B01, pp. 16, 63; [C-B01]

5. Moat / Industry Position

Moat and peers

The credible mechanism is qualification plus engineering familiarity. The offer document says buyers pre-approve suppliers before sending enquiries. Integrated forging, heat treatment and machining, certification claims including ISO 9001, PED and NORSOK, and repeat orders make an approved vendor more useful than a generic job shop. However, certificates are entry qualifications, not patents; customers supply the drawings, negotiate each batch, and can qualify alternatives. The June call acknowledges smaller specialists competing at product level and harming conversion. No audited retention, share-of-wallet, exclusive contract, customer switching-cost study or sustained incremental return premium establishes a strong structural moat. MOAT 5 is supported: useful but contestable process/customer relationships, not monopoly economics. B03, PDF p. 97; B01, pp. 9, 13; B02, 28:05–29:05

7. Growth Quality & Reinvestment Runway

Runway

The near-term opportunity is to fill the newly commissioned machining plant, improve throughput of the existing three-shift system, add difficult geometries with the five-axis machine, and convert qualified export prospects. Management's June estimate of ₹140–150 crore annual revenue at current facilities implies 44.6–54.9% monetary headroom from FY26 revenue. It is a mix/price-dependent management ceiling, not rated tonnage or guaranteed demand. At 20% annual growth the ₹140 crore boundary is reached in roughly two years; a five-year 20% path needs additional capacity or a materially different product mix. B01, pp. 14, 18; B02, 12:20–14:04; [C-B02]

June management guidance contemplated ₹2–3 crore FY27 machine/inspection capex, still being finalized, and said Plant III had 30–35% unused space. Space is not installed, qualified or funded equipment. Guidance for 20–25% or higher FY27 growth is a management scenario, not a model base case. The later chairman's message prioritizes measurement/material identification, expanded NORSOK qualifications and railway exploration; none carries a disclosed revenue commitment. B02, 07:29–08:23, 16:57–17:20, 32:08–34:50; B01, p. 18

A German customer's regular orders reportedly began in February 2026, with approximately one million annual business indicated on the June call and another German customer at pilot stage. The transcript is not a binding customer contract; do not capitalize the entire indicated pipeline or assume pilot qualification at the cutoff. The June order book was only about 20–22% of the prior year's sales, with six-to-ten-week fulfilment. That supports short-cycle activity, not multi-year revenue visibility. B02, 21:49–23:03, 26:03–26:42

DUR 6 is appropriate: installed assets and identifiable qualification avenues provide accessible runway, but oil-and-gas cyclicality, concentrated OEM demand, short orders and capacity requirements limit visibility. Model demand shortfall, delayed qualification, raw-material inflation and slower customer pickups together. Ex-works terms may reduce direct freight exposure but do not protect order timing or collections when buyers face shipping disruption. B01, p. 17; B02, 23:22–25:04

The growth-quality question is whether additional customer orders can fund their inventory, receivables and machinery while earning adequate returns per share. Revenue growth, margin recovery and reported CFO answer different parts of that question. Do not substitute a forecast capacity ramp for REV, or a one-year supplier-credit increase for durable CASH. The retained paths explicitly link demand, capacity, collections, supplier terms, maintenance and expansion spending, debt repayment and shareholder claims. They establish conditional cash requirements rather than validate management’s revenue ambition.

Shri Balaji: reproducible linked funding and valuation methodology

Scope and status

This is a conditional starting-state experiment, not an executable recommendation or a claim that the company is insolvent. Historical anchors are the standalone FY 2025–26 accounts audited on 23 May 2026, available before the 20 October 2026 information cutoff. The market reference is the parent research's secondary 19 October close of ₹134; no primary close or executable quote is claimed. Issued shares are 8,160,000. Nominal INR, pre-personal-tax returns; default equity hurdle 15%, with 12% and 18% sensitivities. WACC 14% is a separate analyst assumption, not mechanically the investor hurdle.

The model deliberately does not manufacture a 31 October balance from a 31 March balance. It projects 60 monthly periods, November 2026–October 2031, using the older balance solely to expose operating/funding dependencies. The ten-year experiment extends to October 2036. No unverified interim cash, debt repayment, fresh loan draw, IPO proceeds, issuance or post-cutoff event is inserted. Accordingly `valuation_eligible=false` for every case; R, D and P cannot be scored from these outputs as though they were source-underwritten current valuations. A failed modeled path is a conditional cash shortfall, not an evidenced G 3 failure.

Monthly engine and accounting identity

For each month:

1. Annual demand is prior annual demand times the selected annual growth. Monthly demand is one twelfth. Sales are the smaller of demand and the applicable revenue ceiling, divided by 12. Demand continues to compound even when capacity clips sales. This flat intra-year profile creates an immediate working-capital step at the beginning of each year; it is a conservative timing stress, not evidence of seasonal monthly sales. A verified seasonal profile could materially change the first funding-failure month.

2. EBITDA=sales×margin. Depreciation is the lesser of active opening PPE and annual depreciation rate/12×active PPE. Paid opening CWIP and pre-commission construction spending transfer into active PPE once at commissioning; no second cash charge is imposed for already paid CWIP. Maintenance capex is 4% of sales; growth capex is₹4,4,3,3,3 crore across five years. Before commissioning growth capex enters CWIP; after commissioning it is assumed immediately productive. No claimed disclosed maintenance estimate is inferred from depreciation.

3. EBIT=EBITDA−D&A. Interest is 10%/12 of opening gross debt in the base case; tax is 25.5% of positive EBT, with no loss tax asset or subsidy rescue. PAT=EBT−tax.

4. Receivables=annualized monthly sales×receivable days/365; inventory and payables use annualized monthly materials-cost proxy×their respective days/365; other ONWC is 1.5% of annualized monthly sales. The full change from opening ONWC is a cash use/release. Receivables, inventory and payables remain separately visible in every retained row.

5. Cash before financing=opening cash+PAT+D&A−cash capex−change ONWC−scheduled principal. Incremental debt is limited to the scenario's explicit maximum, zero in main cases. Hypothetical borrowing is not represented as sanctioned. An explicit hypothetical equity case contributes cash once, at a stated issue price, and increases diluted shares once.

6. Only cash above the₹0.5 crore floor can be distributed at year-end; desired payout is a fraction of positive annual PAT. Dividend payments reduce terminal cash and enter shareholder IRR separately.

7. Cash, debt, PPE, CWIP and shares carry forward without resetting. Once cash becomes negative the path fails. It remains full-length to diagnose cumulative funding needs; later recovery does not retroactively finance the earlier gap. Negative cash is not relabeled actual debt or an approved rescue facility.

Debt principal in the first 12 months includes bank current maturity₹8.3518197 crore and current related-party debt₹1 crore. Remaining disclosed bank/other long-term debt₹4.8565267 crore is spread over the next three years as an assumption, not a reconstructed loan-wise contract. Long-term related-party debt₹8.6281264 crore and demand-bank debt₹0.119493 crore remain in terminal debt rather than disappearing. A recall scenario demands the long-term related-party balance in month 1. Its actual callable terms, rollover support and interest are not established. The base 10% all-debt interest assumption can differ from reported expense because the latter reflects intrayear balances and unresolved related-party terms.

Valuation, distributions and perimeters

The multiple method values terminal enterprise at positive EBITDA×exit multiple, then subtracts actual modeled debt and other senior claims, adds retained cash and separately carried mutual funds. For a failed path, signed negative cash is deducted as an explicitly unfunded diagnostic claim, not silently clipped to zero. Common equity is floored at zero. The bridge divided by forecast diluted shares gives diagnostic terminal price. `terminal_price_investable` and `irr_investable` are null unless both funding and source-eligibility conditions hold; none holds for current valuation here.

Annual cash-flow IRR uses initial−₹134, each year-end per-share dividend and final dividend+terminal price. It uses annual equal periods, not XIRR with invented actual distribution dates. Return costs, spreads and impact are omitted because no reliable execution model is available; the parent liquidity review addresses this separately. No probabilities are assigned, averaged or implied.

FCFF=EBIT−tax on positive operating EBIT+D&A−capex−change ONWC. The cross-check discounts explicit FCFF at WACC, then uses next-year terminal NOPAT and perpetual reinvestment `(terminal growth/terminal ROIC)×terminal NOPAT`. Terminal FCFF=NOPAT−reinvestment; terminal value=FCFF/(WACC−growth), with growth below WACC. Opening debt and senior claims are deducted and opening usable cash/non-operating investments added exactly once. This is a highly assumption-dependent perpetuity; terminal ROIC 15% is not verified. Value may be mostly terminal, or the terminal share can exceed 100% if explicit-period FCFF is negative. It is not a statistical probability.

For hypothetical future equity issuance, FCFF DCF per-share price is NA: an existing-holder FCFE perimeter incorporating timing/proceeds has not been built. The terminal EV/share method does capture issuance cash and dilution. In other cases DCF divides by opening issued shares. The model does not mix terminal diluted shares with time-zero equity DCF without adjusting the proceeds.

11. Bear Case & Permanent-Loss Path

The strongest bear case is that better reported revenue does not translate into a sufficiently profitable, self-financing increase in manufacturing throughput. Raw-material price pass-through can raise nominal sales while customer negotiations delay orders; a major customer reducing purchases can leave qualified machinery underused. Slower receivables and inventory absorption then coincide with suppliers demanding cash and bank principal falling due. Maintenance and committed construction cannot all be deferred without damaging future output. With little verified unrestricted opening cash, a financing gap can force expensive debt, discounted equity or asset sales. Minority shareholders suffer through lower earnings, dilution and a lower terminal valuation, even if reported accounting profit stays positive.

The thesis-break variable is recurring cash generation after necessary operating reinvestment and unavoidable debt service, not the quoted share price. The full monthly model preserves failed paths and peak unfunded deficits rather than silently raising a rescue. Those paths are conditional stress experiments from a stale FY26 balance sheet; they are not evidence that the company has defaulted or that the modeled cash gap occurred. Counterevidence is the audited positive operating earnings and cash generation and the real installed manufacturing base. These support continued research, but supplier funding, restricted deposits and financial presentation issues must be resolved before concluding resilience. Permanent-loss risk is High on a conditional basis because customer/working-capital stress and refinancing can reinforce one another; the probability and actual recovery are not estimated. 12. 5× / 10× Feasibility

A 5× or 10× outcome is Speculative / unsupported on the available evidence, not ruled out arithmetically. At ₹134, a 5× terminal price is ₹670 and a 10× price ₹1,340, before dividends. Required annualized price appreciation is 38.0% and 58.5% over five years, or 17.5% and 25.9% over ten. The price decomposition is revenue growth × attributable net-margin change × opening/terminal diluted shares × terminal/opening P/E; dilution must reduce the per-share result and dividends are separate. At unchanged margins, shares and multiples, business revenue itself must rise 5× or 10×—from ₹96.8071 crore to about ₹484.0 crore or ₹968.1 crore. These are required outcomes, not accessible capacity forecasts. An expansion announcement does not establish the qualified capacity, customer demand, working-capital finance or incremental returns needed.
