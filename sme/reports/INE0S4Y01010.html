1. Executive Verdict

Shree Karni Fabcom is a small B2B technical-fabric manufacturer and sourcing business whose integrated weaving, knitting, coating and dyeing capabilities offer a useful but contestable position. The investable thesis is an execution-sensitive manufacturing/redeployment situation, not an evidenced hold-forever compounder. Revenue has grown, but cash absorption and returns on the enlarged capital base have lagged accounting profit. The stitching-unit closure approved in June 2026 supersedes the earlier finished-goods growth story; existing fabric operations must justify the capital already committed. Completed promoter warrant conversions support financing but do not demonstrate that gross proceeds remain freely available. At the dated ₹418 close, the report separates scoreable historical quality from conditional valuation experiments and unresolved implementation evidence. Better cash conversion, a verified post-conversion funding bridge and profitable redeployment would matter more than promotional customer names or a lower quoted P/E.

4. Business Overview

Business overview

Legal identity and economic perimeter

Shree Karni Fabcom Limited is the listed issuer, ISIN INE0S4Y01010, NSE symbol SHREEKARNI, CIN L47820GJ2023PLC140106. Its antecedent LLP was incorporated on 7 March 2018; commercial operations began in 2019, conversion to a company occurred on 11 April 2023, and it became public on 20 October 2023. Listing followed on 14 March 2024. This is a young operating and public-market history, not a long-cycle proof of compounder economics. SKFL-P; SKFL-AR26

The business manufactures and supplies technical fabrics used in luggage, backpacks, footwear and apparel lining, medical arch support, chairs and upholstery. It is a B2B producer: the end consumer normally buys a customer's product, not a Shree Karni-branded fabric. The economic activity combines yarn procurement, fabric production, processing, finishing and customer-specific delivery. The parent performs knitting and now dyeing; IGK Technical Textiles LLP supplies important weaving/coating/sizing/embossing capabilities. The parent owns 66.67% of IGK. Textron Fabtech LLP is a 45%-owned associate (FY26 AOC1, printed p37/PDF40), not a wholly owned plant whose cash belongs entirely to listed shareholders. Consolidated operating performance must therefore be separated from owner-attributable profit and distributable cash. Eliminating intercompany transactions is essential. SKFL-P pp128–134; SKFL-AR26 pp36–42 and financial notes

This is physical manufacturing with meaningful fixed assets, inventory and receivable funding. It is not an asset-light platform, a contracted utility or an EPC order-book business. It carries raw-material costs and customer collection risk even when incremental demand can use installed machinery. Procurement purchase orders and forecasts are not equivalent to long-duration take-or-pay contracts.

How customers decide and what creates cash

A buyer evaluates fabric strength, colour consistency, coating quality, waterproof or other performance attributes, approved specifications, delivery reliability and price. Process coordination and quality control can make a qualified supplier useful. The economic benefit accrues only if that usefulness translates into repeat orders at prices covering raw material, conversion, depreciation, finance costs and collection risk. Famous customer logos alone do not establish pricing power or exclusivity.

The prospectus identifies a highly competitive, fragmented industry and purchase-order based selling without long-term customer contracts. The company also lacked a formal binding service agreement with IGK at IPO and did not generally have binding raw-material supply contracts. Integration lowers coordination exposure but does not make polyester or nylon prices controllable. The absence of long contracts need not prevent a good manufacturing business, but it changes the duration of the cash-flow claim: each selling cycle has to be earned again. SKFL-P pp31–33,132–134,219

Facility status is not interchangeable with product ambition

Dyeing commenced commercially on 6 April 2025, according to a signed exchange disclosure. A Noida facility commenced on 27 June 2025. These are actual milestones, unlike capacity proposals. However, the board approved closure of the stitching facility on 24 June 2026, with completion expected by the end of that month. FY26 turnover of the unit was ₹16.29 crore, 8.03% of the stated company turnover, and net worth ₹10.47 crore, 9.60%. The disclosure says a sale agreement is not applicable. Those are economic scale figures, not cash-sale proceeds. Do not silently retain closed stitching capacity in a growth model or assume its net worth is realizable cash. 5. Moat and Industry Position

Moat and industry position

Integration: a useful capability, not a monopoly

Mechanism: weaving/knitting, coating and dyeing coordination can reduce outsourced lead times and rejects and improve specification control. Evidence: operating IGK production, commercial dyeing commissioning and a multi-process portfolio. Durability: these facilities and trained processes take capital and time to replicate. Failure mode: capable competing mills can buy similar equipment; excess industry capacity or customer dual-sourcing can pass savings to buyers. Public evidence does not quantify a persistent cost gap against the next-best supplier. Therefore integration supports a contestable operating advantage, not an automatic high-moat score. SKFL-P; SKFL-PPT25; SKFL-DYE25

Qualification: stronger evidence than logos, weaker than guaranteed revenue

The signed 27 June 2025 exchange announcement states that Shree Karni cleared Walmart's audit and became an approved vendor in India. In November 2025 management discussed Walmart and Target vendor codes and hoped these customers could contribute 20% of business in the following year, conditional on tariff conditions. Approval demonstrates a capability threshold. It does not disclose minimum purchases, duration, exclusivity, price protection, margins or cash advances. The June 2026 stitching closure undermines a straight-line extrapolation of the finished-product strategy without proving vendor approval itself was revoked. A responsible report preserves both the qualification achievement and the subsequently changed monetization route. SKFL-WALMART25; SKFL-CALL25 pp15–17; SKFL-CLOSE26

Concentration and switching

FY23 top-five customer concentration was 43.54%; top ten were 61.12%. For the eight months ended November 2023, corresponding figures were 60.30% and 74.23%. These are dated concentration measures, not FY26 customer shares. The partial-period increase may reflect seasonality as well as concentration, but it leaves insufficient evidence for broad customer diversification. FY23 Maharashtra contributed 43.02% and Gujarat 26.44%, with the top five states accounting for 84.78%. FY23 top-ten suppliers were 46.97% of purchases. Current customer/supplier concentration and binding contract duration remain open items. SKFL-P pp31–33,132–134

A customer can value reliable delivery and still negotiate aggressively. No verified market-share denominator establishes national leadership in the exact product set. Management's narrow positioning claims must not be expanded into a general technical-textiles market monopoly. Retention, price resets and rejection data over several years would be more probative than a customer logo page.

Comparative moat judgment

Nobletex is a closer process/product comparator than a civil contractor. Mayur Uniquoters is an adjacent coated-fabric producer whose automotive qualification and export mix make its economics informative but not directly transferable. SRF's coated/laminated-fabric operations are relevant, while SRF's chemicals earnings are not. Garware's specialized application development illustrates what deeper intellectual-property/customer-solution differentiation can look like, but it is not a like-for-like luggage-fabric peer. The separate peer module specifies period, scope and unavailable cash data rather than assigning unverified multiples.

Suggested MOAT: 5/10, medium confidence. Useful integration and demonstrated vendor qualification coexist with replicable machinery, concentrated purchase-order demand and unproven sustained pricing advantage. A score of 7 would require clearer evidence of superior retained economics and switching costs, not simply larger installed capacity.

Driver bridge

1. Production volume increased as woven capacity and utilization expanded.

2. Vertical integration added dyeing capability and potential savings; it does not automatically add an equal amount of third-party consolidated revenue because internal processing must be eliminated.

3. Higher-value nylon or specialty products can lift rupees per metre, but this is mix, not the same as physical volume.

4. Finished bags added a new business line that was subsequently curtailed through stitching closure.

5. Contract manufacturing may support revenue without identical owned-machine output, while requiring supplier quality control and working capital.

6. Imported/distributed technical knits under the Italian partnership create a different gross-margin and capital-intensity mix from proprietary manufacturing.

Management said in November 2025 that product prices could range from approximately ₹30 fabric to ₹100 nylon and that additional revenue could come with relatively few machines. This is evidence that product mix and bottlenecks matter, not a valid universal realized selling price. Applying one price to all weaving, coating and dyeing throughput would double-count material moving through several stages. SKFL-CALL25 pp6,17

7. Growth Quality and Reinvestment Runway

Runway and demand durability

Addressable demand versus bankable demand

Luggage, footwear, upholstery and functional apparel offer multiple applications. Travel and replacement demand can expand over time; premium or recycled materials can change product mix. Nevertheless, global technical-textiles TAM is much broader than the products, certifications, geography and customer channels this company can actually address. No defensible exact company market share or five-year accessible market denominator was located. Demand breadth earns some runway credit but not a guaranteed growth trajectory.

In November 2025 management described luggage as 50–60% of revenue and targeted no industry above 30% by FY27/FY28. This is a diversification goal, not a current result. Three-to-six-month customer forecasts are not binding backlog. Responsive delivery requires ready inventory and therefore financing before customers pay. Management's ₹300 crore revenue possibility included contract manufacturing; it is not a verified owned-capacity ceiling or a funded order book. SKFL-CALL25 pp5,15,17

Italian agency: access, not owned intellectual property

The 6 May 2026 announcement describes an exclusive arrangement to promote, brand and sell Italian technical knitted fabrics in India. The annual report identifies YEGOF of the Carvico Group. This may broaden access to nylon/spandex and polyester/spandex products. The partner's famous customers do not automatically become direct Shree Karni customers. Minimum purchases, commission schedule, margin, duration and termination economics were not verified. This is a real commercial avenue with unquantified earnings, not a contracted annuity. SKFL-ITALY26; SKFL-AR26 p6

Terminal value and returns

Every financially funded horizon is followed by at least 36 months of cash testing, extended for any later specified maturity. The terminal years continue maintenance, working capital, cash claims and debt repayment without new hypothetical flexible credit. Thus an apparently attractive year-five balance cannot conceal a forced repayment a few months later. A future cash reserve is retained, and only excess horizon cash is added to the enterprise-to-common-equity bridge.

The exit-multiple cross-check capitalizes earned last-year operating EBITDA, then subtracts all horizon debt, unpaid other claims and the explicit NCI claim, and adds only excess cash and separately modeled non-operating recoveries. This is not a liquidation guarantee.

The FCFF DCF is separate. DCF deducts changes in the required operating cash reserve from each monthly operating FCFF, uses the signed initial cash less required reserve adjustment, so an initial reserve deficit is funded once rather than clipped away, and includes the greater of observed terminal reserve increase and growth-required reserve investment in the perpetual envelope. Its terminal cash flow is the lower of actual next-year operating FCFF and a normalized envelope: next-year NOPAT plus depreciation, less the greater of actual/replacement maintenance, less the greater of actual/growth-required capex, less growth-required operating working capital. A temporary stock release cannot create a perpetual cash windfall. Applied terminal growth is capped by actual next-year realized revenue growth; a capacity-bound flat-sales case receives zero terminal growth even if the scenario originally requested 3%.

DCF enterprise cash flows exclude financing. The bridge separately subtracts opening debt and other claims; it applies signed opening cash minus the required reserve, including any opening reserve deficit, then adds the stated associate value and fund net present value after funding commitments. Hypothetical future net equity proceeds are discounted and added once, and the resulting claim is divided by the corresponding fully diluted share count. DCF results are conditional capital-structure experiments, not observed fair prices. The multiple exit and DCF are alternative cross-checks, never summed.

All returns include 1% assumed entry and 1% exit friction, before personal taxes. These assumptions are not measured bid-ask spreads. IRR uses dated monthly dividend distributions and final exit proceeds. Hurdle-price tests use 12%, 15% and 18% nominal equity hurdles. The WACC is separately tested at 12%–18%; it is not mechanically the equity hurdle. Growth/multiple, margin/WACC, WACC/terminal-growth and receivable/inventory grids are fully preserved. Reverse expectations report the tested grid and funded crossings, not an invented unique required CAGR when the cash path fails.

The 5×/10× tests cover five and ten years, include capex, working capital, finite debt and hypothetical dilution, and compare achieved per-share outcomes. No tested crossing is a probability or recommendation. Scenarios that grow demand faster than productive capacity show both quantities explicitly.

11. Bear Case and Permanent Loss Path

The strongest bear case is not that technical textiles disappear. It is that useful capabilities fail to earn adequate returns on the larger asset and working-capital base. The closed stitching adjacency provides an observable example of execution and geopolitical risk. Continuing fabric sales can grow while inventories, customer credit and cash tied in expansion rise faster than owner value. In a linked stress, weaker utilization and raw-material pass-through reduce margins, slower collections and inventory losses absorb liquidity, lenders or unsecured creditors require repayment and an equity raise transfers a larger share of future value to new capital. Illiquid fund investments and collateral deposits need not become cash when needed. A depressed share price can amplify dilution and make an existing holding difficult to exit. Scenario reserve breaches are analytical failures under stated assumptions, not evidence of an observed company default. The key falsification variable is sustained cash generation after the full capital and working-capital commitment, not whether one reporting period shows higher sales.

12. Fivefold and Tenfold Feasibility

Fivefold requires about 38.0% annual price growth over five years or 17.5% over ten; tenfold requires about 58.5% or 25.9%, respectively. Price compounding must be distinguished from revenue growth, enterprise value, dilution and dividend distributions.
