4. Business Overview

Athirappilly is an integrated17.38-acre regional attraction with41 rides, separate snow park, restaurants and eight resort rooms. Jamshedpur snow park openedOctober 2025; Lucknow is an expansion project. FY26 resort revenue₹0.3716 crore is under1% of sales. Customers include families, tourists and institutional/school groups. Admission, food, merchandise and ancillary services create revenue; ticket cash normally precedes or accompanies service, explaining low trade receivables. Repeat demand requires fresh attractions, safety and affordability; it is not a subscription annuity. [B01/B02, RHP189–202]

FY26 combined attraction visits6.71 lakh versus5.14 lakh; these are not verified unique customers. Legacy simultaneous capacity/utilization is ND. The ropeway notice claims5,000 daily and15 lakh annual capacity, but neither acreage nor installed throughput demonstrates demand. Customer/supplier concentration by economic group is ND; diverse ticket buyers do not eliminate concentrated school channels, geography or equipment dependencies.

5. Moat and Industry Position

Regional location near Athirappilly waterfalls, installed infrastructure, local distribution and multi-attraction cross-selling create useful advantages. Mechanism → nearby tourism and school access; evidence → established operations since2000 and rising attraction visits; durability → local brand and fixed site; failure → weather, adverse publicity, safety interruption, discounts or competing destinations. This supports MOAT 5, not national pricing power. No defensible local market-share denominator or unique-customer retention series was located. [B01/B02/B11]

Three meaningful primary-document peers challenge the thesis: Wonderla demonstrates greater geographic diversification/new-park execution; Imagicaaworld shows weather and operating-leverage sensitivity despite scale; Nicco shows tenure and exceptional-profit risk despite cash reserves. Exact period/scope and financial comparisons are preserved in the evidence appendix. These are operating comparators, not a fabricated dated P/E ranking. Superior scale does not immunize any park against demand or capex mistakes. [P01–P03]

11. Bear Case and Permanent Loss Path

The critical variable is cash earned by the newly installed attractions relative to remaining cash commitments. A normal share-price correction is not the same as impairment. The linked bear is slower visits/price, delayed24–36 month ramp, largely fixed staffing,20–30%project overrun, maturities/demand obligations, maintenance and tax claims. The five-year bear first fails in modelmonth15 (June 2027), with cumulative diagnostic unmet cash₹9.57 crore across the extended stress horizon. It is not evidence the issuer actually defaults then.

Permanent-loss risk is High: new shareholders can finance assets that fail to earn adequate returns, suffer dilutive recapitalization, or own only a residual after secured lenders/taxes. Group advances may recover less than face value and cannot pay park bills today. No supported distress asset-sale valuation was located; a0/20/40%recovery of entry capital is only a mechanical−100/−80/−60%loss sensitivity, not an estimated recovery or ordinary IRR. Do not assign a normal bear target price after the funding test fails.

Strong contrary evidence: established operations, genuine attendance growth, positive multi-year CFO, partial related-party loan collections, no reported lender default and fresh IPO equity. The standalone auditor’s CARO clause19 (AR26 printed61, audit report dated4 July 2026) adds material counterevidence: on evidence available at that audit date, no material uncertainty was identified about meeting liabilities existing at31 March 2026 that fell due within the following year. The auditor explicitly did not assure future viability or guarantee that every liability would be discharged. This scoped assessment does not verify October liquidity or post-IPO deployment. These facts reduce the case for declaring inevitable failure. Conversely, IPO cash alone does not prove full funding after debt repayment, taxes, fees and projects. The model's extreme demand-debt scenarios are contractual timing stress, not current creditor demands. [B02/B10,F02/F05/F09,M01]

Returns and DCF

FCFF = EBITDA − tax on positive operating EBIT − ΔONWC − cash capex. Debt interest and principal are not FCFF. Cash recurrence separately deducts actual modeled financing interest, taxes, principal, fees and claims; debt/equity flows add only when specified. Negative cash creates permanent failed-path status even if later positive cash appears. Later model rows after default are diagnostic unmet-obligation arithmetic, not a viable business or free cash for shareholders. Failed paths retain all rows and have no ordinary going-concern IRR/DCF/terminal price.

Funded exit EV = terminal trailing EBITDA×multiple; equity adds cash less all debt and1cr operating liquidity reserve. No automatic multiple rerating beyond stated scenario. No base payout. Hypothetical future equity adds net cash, fees and actual new share count, then lowers existing-holder claim through diluted exit/dividends. These paths retain explicit investor IRR, but DCF per-existing-share is NS because no full owner-participation DCF is provided; enterprise FCFF is retained. This is not mislabelled a complete diluted DCF.

DCF uses month-discounted FCFF and next12 month continuing FCFF/(WACC−g). Physical visits freeze after horizon, yield and costs grow atg; growth/replacement capex continues. Post-entry old tax/claims and stress claims are discounted and deducted from equity separately, avoiding omission. Only dated group-loan recovery is added as nonoperating value. No double-add of IPO or new-finance proceeds already inside cash bridge. Terminal value remains highly assumption-dependent; its share is disclosed. Posthorizon changing depreciation/tax shields and capacity ceilings make a constant-g terminal an approximation, not an engineering perpetuity.
