1. Executive Verdict

Shreeji Global FMCG is primarily a business-to-business agricultural sourcing and processing company, not yet a demonstrated consumer-brand compounder. The SHETHJI brand and new flour/blended-spice capacity provide an avenue to improve value addition, while recurring staple demand and historical operating-profit growth are genuine strengths. The decisive risk is cash-supported expansion: FY2026 reported CFO was negative ₹59.74 crore despite PAT of ₹19.92 crore, and earmarked IPO deposits overlap a substantial FD-backed overdraft structure. At the verified ₹180.40 close, reported earnings alone do not establish an attractive investment. BQS, GQS and IAS remain NS where unresolved growth decomposition and current funding evidence prevent defensible point scores. This is an agricultural-processing transformation watchlist, not an evidence-backed compounder. Reconsider after reconciled accounts, deployable project cash and realized product-level sales establish a credible per-share funding and return path. F01; F02; F03; F04; M01

4. Business Overview

Business model: an agricultural processor, not yet a demonstrated consumer franchise

Shreeji Global FMCG Limited (formerly Shreeji Agri Commodity) sources agricultural commodities, cleans/grades/sorts whole seeds, grinds spices and manufactures flour. It sells both SHETHJI-branded and white-label products in wholesale and retail pack sizes. Branding on a bag does not establish consumer pricing power: the prospectus's B2B/B2C table records 100% B2B and zero B2C for FY2023–FY2025 and the five months to August 2025. In FY2025, the buyer table attributes ₹364.3788 crore to wholesale distributors, ₹226.8314 crore to corporate clients, ₹57.6476 crore to export buyers and only ₹0.0636 crore to other retail clients; those are purchaser classifications, not audited own-brand revenue shares. No reliable branded/unbranded or manufacturing/trading revenue split is available. The February 2018 incorporation and January 2025 name change do not turn the historic commodity economics into mature FMCG economics. BUS01 pp 113–121

Money is earned from sourcing, processing, grading, delivery reliability and a modest gross spread on substantial commodity turnover. Procurement through Rajkot/Gondal APMCs and proximity to Unjha/Mundra are useful operating advantages, but competing processors can use the same markets. Spot procurement without long-term supply contracts exposes margins and inventory values to crop prices. Bulk/white-label buyers can bargain and switch. Regulations, contamination/recall risk, weather, export restrictions and FX are operating risks rather than generic checklist entries. BUS01 pp 24–33,123–137

The FY2025 geographic record is concentrated: Gujarat ₹504.8871 crore equals 77.80% of total revenue and 85.39% of domestic sales; exports ₹57.6476 crore are 8.88% of total. UAE accounted for approximately 53.65% of export revenue, not total company sales. The prospectus reports 45 authorised distributors, 44 corporate/modern-trade buyers, 378 bulk buyers and 53 export buyers, 520 in total. A buyer count is not retention, recurring contracted demand, or verified outlet reach. BUS01 pp 120–121,134–137

Customer-risk reconciliation matters. The prose on printed p 32 mislabels top-five percentages as top-ten; the underlying table gives FY2025 top ten ₹203.9738 crore / ₹648.9215 crore =31.43%, top five 21.92%, and largest ₹44.4437 crore / ₹648.9215 crore =6.849%, not the table's 6.58%. Five-month August 2025 top ten 63.05%, top five 48.49%, largest 17.07% show concentration can change sharply. These are not comparable full-year growth rates. The supplier-risk paragraph repeats customer numbers and changes the scope label to consolidated; do not treat it as a verified supplier-concentration series. BUS01 pp 32–33

5. Moat and Industry Position

Moat mechanisms and disconfirmation

MOAT suggestion: 3/10, Medium confidence. The positive mechanism is local sourcing knowledge plus processing consistency, flexible packaging and distribution relationships. Existing grinding/flour investment and quality certifications can improve access and spreads. The contrary evidence is dominant B2B exposure, no measured own-brand price premium or repeat-consumer economics, ordinary spot-procurement access and customers without demonstrated switching barriers. CARE independently describes limited value addition and intense fragmented competition. These support a replicable-processing classification rather than a durable consumer-brand moat. Certifications are market access requirements, not proof of excess returns. BUS01 pp 121–137; BUS03 pp 1–2

SHETHJI has a registered class 30 device mark in the prospectus; other classes were still at application/formality stages. This protects a name/device, not a patented production process or category monopoly. A moat upgrade needs verified premium realisations, branded revenue share, retained distributors/outlet productivity and cash-profitable repeat sales over several years. A rise in total sales or a company name including FMCG is insufficient. BUS01 pp 241–243

6. Revenue Growth and Scale

Growth and per-share reality

FY2026 revenue increased 18.10%, or ₹117.4781 crore, against FY2025 statutory revenue; PAT increased 62.84%. The FY2023–FY2026 revenue CAGR is 17.93% over three actual annual intervals. A five-year CAGR requires FY2021, not FY2022; the FY2021 scope ambiguity prevents treating that older baseline as a clean comparable standalone series. A ten-year comparable CAGR is also ND because eleven comparable annual observations were not available. Revenue additions were ₹120.94 crore in FY2024, ₹60.70 crore in FY2025, and ₹117.48 crore in FY2026: growth has not been uniformly accelerating. F01 printed 183; F02 PDF105; financial_metrics

Statutory operating EBITDA margin, excluding all other income, increased from 2.62% to 3.68%. This strict definition is conservative because other income includes trading-linked FX and export incentives, but avoids silently treating all incidental income as franchise earnings. CARE's PBILDT ₹31.58 crore is a distinct broader definition, not the ₹28.185 crore strict figure. Product-level volume, price, mix and branded-versus-bulk bridges remain incomplete; rising revenue cannot be equated to organic unit growth or premium-brand share gains. F02 PDF106–110; F04 pp 2–3

Share count rose 42.61% from 15.96 million to 22.76 million in the IPO. The FY2025 increase from 5.7 to 15.96 million includes the 1.8-for-1 bonus and is not economic dilution. Historical EPS denominators are inconsistent across vintages, so current-capital owner earnings are more useful for valuation: FY2026 PAT/current 22.76 million shares is ₹8.7524; FY2025 statutory PAT/current shares is ₹5.3750. This is explicitly current-capital pro forma, not weighted-average reported EPS. The five-month August 2025 data is not annualized. F01 printed 193–194; F02 PDF93–96,105

Growth bridge: real plant activity, unresolved economic demand

The product-level facts show operational activity but not a complete organic volume-price bridge. Whole-seed production was 47,677/68,681/60,176 MT in FY2023/24/25; FY2025 output declined 12.38% despite increasing rupee revenue. Ground-spice output rose from 665 MT in the partial-start FY2024 to 3,230 MT in FY2025; flour 113→3,167 MT. These categories have different prices and margins; summing tonnes and calling the result like-for-like demand growth would be misleading. FY2025 reported installed capacities were 91,200/4,000/3,750 MT respectively. The disclosed 67% whole-seed utilisation does not precisely equal 60,176/91,200=65.98%; the document warns that utilisation uses management assumptions. Five-month August 2025 utilisation 93% grinding and 92% flour is prorated, not a full-year record. BUS01 p 134

7. Growth Quality and Reinvestment Runway

Accessible runway and capital demands

DUR suggestion:5/10, Medium confidence. Recurring staple demand, an existing Gujarat sourcing/distribution footprint and demonstrated initial flour/grinding operations provide an addressable runway. The IPO funds a tangible next step: planned 22,500 MT blended spices,37,500 MT multigrain/millet flour,5,000 MT cold storage and 1,000 kW captive solar. The planned flour capacity is 10 times the existing 3,750 MT; blended spices are a new category, not simply another 5.625 times identical current grinding capacity. Capacity is supply, not orders. Distribution build-out, pricing, food safety, sustained utilization and inventory financing determine accessible growth. BUS01 pp 82–91,126–134

The prospectus allocated net proceeds ₹82.4697 crore: factory ₹5.6741, machinery/cold storage ₹29.0076, solar ₹4.2480, working capital ₹33.54 and general purposes ₹10.00. Orders had not been placed at prospectus date; regulatory approvals dependent on completed premises remained future requirements. BUS01 pp 82–86

The October 12 CARE rationale envisaged December 2026 operation and growth contribution from FY2028. These are forecasts, not commissioned capacity or secured offtake. Its ₹50 crore rated facilities and low March 2026 gearing are financing counterevidence, but a rated quantum is not current unused sanctioned cash. The body describes 93% historical utilization of ₹20 crore limits and only about ₹2 crore free March cash excluding earmarked capex. A credible upside requires branded/processed mix gains with cash collection and replacement/expansion capex funded. A failure chain is crop-price inflation plus inventory accumulation, customer slowdown, delayed commissioning, fixed-cost underabsorption and refinancing/dilution. Do not model higher margins without the capex, distributor support, working capital, debt and dilution necessary to earn them. Capital returns and reinvestment economics

FY2026 strict EBIT/average(equity + gross debt) is 22.78%; PAT/average equity is 24.64%. These are book-capital ROCE and ROE proxies, not precise post-tax ROIC. The IPO occurred late in the year, materially changing average capital, and book cash includes earmarked project funds while FDs are partly lien-backed. A point-estimate WACC spread would overstate precision. After-tax strict NOPAT at an explicitly assumed 26% tax is ₹20.0241 crore. A transparent gross operating-capital proxy, AR + inventory + supplier/GST operating assets − AP − customer advances − salary payable + net PPE/intangibles, rises ₹61.3313→₹144.0834 crore. At 26% tax, FY2026 NOPAT/average proxy capital is 19.50%, while incremental NOPAT/incremental capital is 9.98%. These exclude both earmarked FDs and corresponding free-cash assumptions from operating IC, retain construction in PPE, and do not substitute debt netting. A fully normalized IC denominator still requires a reliable tax, advance, restricted-cash and project classification bridge. CAP therefore need not inherit the apparently high headline ROE. Failure definition and dividends

Minimum available cash is the greater of ₹1 crore and 0.05 month of operating cash costs; 0.25/0.5-month sensitivities test resilience. This small base reserve is an explicit policy assumption, not a lender covenant. A breach records the first month, reserve shortfall and maximum later shortfall. A simulated breach is not an assertion that the company defaulted. Every failed path retains all later counterfactual months and all contractual financing transitions. Failed paths receive no ordinary terminal valuation or return; negative bank balances in later diagnostic rows are unfinanceable residuals, not assumed overdrafts.

Annual dividends equal at most 10% of actual summed annual PAT and only available cash above reserve. Dividends leave bank cash and are divided by shares outstanding when paid. They are not also retained in terminal value. No future dividend is guaranteed by the ₹0.25 FY2026 declaration.

Return, DCF and terminal consistency

The earnings cross-check uses terminal EBITDA × 7/10/12× for bear/base/bull, less all remaining debt, other claims and any specified senior/NCI claims, plus only cash above the required operating reserve. Negative equity is floored at zero. All shares issued in each path enter the terminal denominator. Entry and exit each incur 1% execution friction. IRR uses monthly-period date offsets from the November start (not day-exact XIRR from the 19 October quote), and solves annual dividends and terminal sale proceeds relative to that cost-inclusive entry, before personal taxes. Hurdle entry prices are separately computed at 12%, 15% and 18%; they are assumption-dependent, not executable bids or observed fair values.

FCFF is actual modeled collections plus operating advances less suppliers, operating costs, other operating-asset growth, operating cash tax and all capex. The increase in necessary cash reserve is deducted as additional operating reinvestment. The DCF discounts these monthly cash flows at a nominal INR WACC of 15% (12%/18% tested), separately estimated from the 15% investor hurdle. An illustrative analytical capital mix of 75% equity at 18% cost and 25% debt at 10% pretax cost, with 26% tax, gives 75% × 18% + 25% × 10% × (1 − 26%) = 15.35%, rounded to 15% for scenario work. This is not an observed capital structure, empirical beta estimate or measured market WACC; financing and tax-shield availability remain uncertain, so the range matters more than the point. It retains at least 36 months of post-horizon funding transition so new-credit maturities cannot disappear into a terminal value.

Normalized mature terminal FCFF subtracts replacement maintenance, incremental fixed assets, incremental operating working capital and cash reserve at the terminal-growth rate. Growth is at most the declared 3% (0–4% sensitivity), constrained by actual modeled growth, and is always below WACC. The full transition's discounted FCFF plus normalized terminal value is bridged to common equity with initial debt/claims, signed opening cash less reserve, actual conditional equity proceeds, and release of previously excluded/earmarked assets only when modeled. Negative opening reserve adjustment is not clipped into free cash. New equity proceeds and terminal diluted shares are both included; it is not free financing. Financing fees and crystallized claims are deducted separately. The reported terminal fraction shows reliance on distant value.

The strongest bear case is not disappearance of demand for food staples: it is inability to retain cash while purchasing and processing those staples.
