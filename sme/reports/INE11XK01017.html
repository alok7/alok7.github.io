Reported FY26 sales of ₹432.37 crore and PAT of ₹24.75 crore look strong, but operating cash outflow of ₹16.50 crore, inventory-funded growth, gold-price/mix distortion and unresolved comparative-account changes prevent an evidence-backed compounder designation. Hold — unresolved; BQS NS, GQS NS and IAS NS. This is an unproven growth-and-working-capital thesis, not a proven fraud/default case. The ₹139.06 reference close is dated 19 October 2026 and secondary corroborated, not a verified executable BSE quote. Independent inventory assurance, reconciled accounts and actual post-IPO cash/use of funds could change the conclusion. [E01,E04,E06]

4. Business Overview

The company designs jewellery in-house and supplies wholesalers, retailers, boutiques and exhibitions through job workers. Product families include heritage, bridal, Jadtar, Polki and Meenakari. The economic activity is designing, sourcing gold, commissioning skilled work, holding inventory and collecting customer cash—not merely collecting a low-capital royalty. The absence of a factory makes fixed assets small but does not make working capital small. [E01: business150–160]

FY25 sales were74.37% Gujarat, with the largest customer26.39% and top10 customers62.43%. Nine-month December2025 concentration was73.31% Gujarat, top customer14.06%, top10 60.46%; diversification improved at the top but remains narrow. These are different reporting lengths, not proof of sustainable deconcentration. Customer count fell from400 FY24 to229 FY25 even as average billed sales per customer rose; this conflicts with a simple broad-volume expansion story. [E01: KPI128/business160]

FY26 product disclosure shows ₹428.2114crore manufactured/design-led and ₹4.1551crore traded sales. “Manufactured” includes outsourced production; it does not establish an owned plant. Product note totals and separately reported other-operating revenue need minor reconciliation. Design and price/mix changes can grow gross sales without proportional grams. [E04: note17]

5. Moat / Industry Position

Relationships with artisans and recurring customers, design refreshes and exhibitions can support a niche. Their defensibility is limited: designs are not registered, no long-term exclusive demand or audited market share was established, and customer concentration creates bargaining risk. Claims of heritage/awards are not a moat score. The proposed studio may improve customer experience, but committing shareholder capital to a promoter-owned site introduces return and governance questions. [E01: risk18/business/objects]

11. Bear Case & Permanent-Loss Path

The strongest bear case is that apparent growth mainly reflects gold prices, higher-ticket mix and inventory/accounting marks, while saleable stock and collections cannot fund obligations. Customer loss then lower margin, slower stock turnover, delayed supplier recovery and depleted IPO liquidity can require dilutive funding at the worst time. The failed model shows why simply discounting a terminal profit stream is insufficient: survival fails first. [E01,E04,A01]

The strongest counterevidence is equally important: genuine disclosed IPO cash, rising operating earnings, audited unmodified statements, a specific studio commencement event, lower single-customer concentration in the December stub, and prior historical cash releases. These do not establish that the bearish story will happen. The thesis-break variable is independently realizable inventory plus collections relative to cash commitments—not a short-term price fall. Missing monitor access does not prove misuse or default.

The failed-path recovery worksheet discounts opening inventory to60%, receivables70% and supplier advances50%, deducting operating payables/customer advances/debt/tax provisions. Its₹8.73per-share residual is an illustrative recovery stress, not liquidation value: other claims, enforcement costs, gold price, priority and current cash are unknown. Equity could lose substantially more; no floor is promised.
