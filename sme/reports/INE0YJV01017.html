1. Executive Verdict

Hold pending financial reliability, deployment and funding evidence. Sheel Biotech is a hybrid agri-horticulture supplier and project operator, not a pharmaceutical biotechnology company. It sells planting material and tissue-culture plants and undertakes landscaping, greenhouse, organic-program and FPO work. 4. Business Overview

The company combines several activities with different cash cycles. Tissue culture starts with biological material, multiplies and hardens it, then supplies plants to agricultural/institutional customers. The disclosed production sequence takes about 142 days before packing, leaving exposure to contamination, rejection, weather during hardening and demand timing. Imported premium blueberry genetics and planting material also introduce supplier/licensing and foreign-exchange exposure. Physical capacity claims are not actual saleable yield or utilization. PROSPECTUS

Greenhouse/packhouse and landscaping revenue follows project awards, execution, certification and collection. Government organic and FPO work depends on program allocation, beneficiary delivery and documentary approval. A government counterparty may reduce some credit-loss risk without making payment prompt or eliminating acceptance disputes. Management describes tender activity early in the fiscal year and material invoicing after later approval, including the March quarter. The model therefore preserves seasonal cash deficits instead of spreading annual profit evenly or annualizing a strong half-year. PROSPECTUS RESULTS_CLARIFICATION

Blueberry economics must be separated: selling planting material, constructing/managing farms for customers, and owning or participating in crop returns are not the same revenue. For owned farms, biological yield, grade, harvest, cold chain, realized price and continuing maintenance matter. For turnkey sales, margin and customer collection matter. For investor-managed farms, revenue-sharing obligations and who owns the produce/assets matter. The 14 July 2026 presentation distinguishes these models; none permits treating a current promotional ROI claim as company revenue or shareholder return. PRESENTATION

The growth path must fund plant production before sale, project labor/materials before approval, and receivables after invoicing. Large ONWC therefore makes sales growth capital hungry even where the fixed-asset base looks small. Calculation and reconstruction notes

Sheel Biotech: conditional monthly cash and valuation specification

Scope and decisive limitation

This is a deterministic experiment, not a live forecast or evidence of available finance. It applies the complete Small/Micro-Cap Master Prompt v 3.0, especially sections 5, 6.5–6.7, 9, 12–14. Historical inputs are INR crore; prices are rupees per share; share counts are actual whole shares. All arithmetic uses Decimal at 40 significant digits, without binary floats. Noninteger powers use Decimal's deterministic precision-limited exponentiation.

The market reference is 19 October 2026; the last located actual trade was 15 October at ₹62.50. The latest financial opening is 31 March 2026. Each path explicitly carries those March balances unchanged to November 2026. The intervening April–October cash, investment, debt and share bridge is missing. Therefore no path is source-eligible; source_eligible_return is null and live R/IAS are NS, even when a conditional funding path finishes. A model cash failure is NOT evidence that G3 fails or the company defaults.

Five-year experiments run November 2026–October 2031; ten-year experiments through October 2036. Annual outputs are November–October anniversary years, not forecasts of reported Indian fiscal years. The source financial ledger stays separate.

Files and interfaces

- model_inputs.json: historical opening facts, source locators, explicit missingness and dated price. Numbers are decimal strings; share counts are integers.

- model.py: load_inputs(path=None), default_scenario(), run(inputs, scenario, years, path_id), summarize(path, kind). run is pure and returns a complete JSON-compatible dictionary. It performs no unrecorded subsidiary runs and no writes.

- scenarios.py: make_scenarios(inputs); specifications(inputs) yields (path_id, scenario_dict, years, kind); target_brackets(summaries, inputs) summarizes all retained search trials; refinement_specifications(summaries, inputs, subdivisions=10) yields additional, unique first-crossing interval tests for a caller to run and retain.

- model_audit.py: audit_path(path, inputs) and audit_suite(iterable_full_paths, inputs). These independently reconcile retained paths; they do not run hidden paths. If no paths argument is supplied, audit_suite reads neighboring canonical_paths.json.gz as a JSON array or a dict with paths.

Each returned path contains scenario, monthly, annual, funding, terminal, failure, and event_rejections. Failed paths retain their failed month and every remaining calendar month marked not_projected_after_failure. Their terminal is null and ordinary return calculations are suppressed. The parent builds canonical_paths.json.gz and trial/annual summaries from every returned path, including failures and reverse-search trials. Five initial development experiments are retained separately under private_qa as QA evidence.

Demand, seasonality and capacity

The business is a hybrid of plants/imports, government landscape/organic/FPO projects, greenhouse construction and blueberry models. No disclosed FY26 segment mix, plant output, plant ASP, crop yield or physical utilization is invented. For trace attribution only, FY25 segment shares are applied to FY26- scaled ordinary revenue; these illustrative segments do not establish a bottom-up capacity proof.

Nominal annual demand = FY26 revenue × (1 + scenario growth)^anniversary year, less any modeled disposed-business revenue. The monthly calendar weights are:

Calendar monthsStandard conditional weights
January / February / March15% / 18% / 22%
April / May / June / July3% / 3% / 3% / 3%
August / September4% / 4%
October / November / December7% / 8% / 10%

Thus April–September is 20% and fiscal Q4 is 55%, consistent in shape, not exact monthly measurement, with government milestone seasonality. Extreme-Q4 weights raise January–March to 70%; an even-month counterfactual shows the importance of timing. No weak or strong half-year is mechanically annualized.

A nominal revenue-capacity index begins at 110% of FY26 revenue. Fulfilled revenue is the lesser of demand and the commissioned nominal index, then multiplied by yield and price factors. Growth capex creates capacity only after the stated commissioning delay. This is an explicitly assumed capex-productivity index, not a physical plant-capacity model. The prospectus 10 million plants and later management 15 million claim remain separate observations; neither becomes saleable volume. Actual physical ramp, plant mix and tender capacity are critical missing evidence, so even an arithmetically funded path cannot be labeled supported.

Materials equal pre-price, pre-yield fulfilled value × assumed 62% material ratio. Yield loss reduces saleable revenue without removing the input materials cost. Opex is calibrated to the scenario target EBITDA margin under full un-stressed utilization: 40% is fixed across calendar months and 60% follows fulfilled value. Price cuts do not proportionally erase costs. Customer loss, yield, price, margins, slow receipts and commissioning delays are linked in stress cases.

Receipts, unbilled balances and inventory

New government share is assumed 56.63% of ordinary sales, based on FY25 tender exposure, not verified FY26 mix. In the base experiment, 20% of government revenue first enters unbilled; billing occurs three months later, then payment six months after billing. Direct government bills have six-month collection lag, private bills three months. Opening current AR is collected equally over nine months. Opening >one-year AR is collected 85% over months 10–24, with the uncollected 15% impaired at month one. Opening unbilled bills equally over months 3–8, followed by government payment lag. Every timing/recovery assumption is A.

Government/customer delay scenarios change both opening recovery and new receivables. Actual age-by-milestone collection evidence remains missing. The October Himachal ₹4.54 crore and July Jammu/Kashmir ₹21.39 crore award notices add neither revenue nor cash: they may already be inside the demand path, and lack executable billing/cash terms.

Customer advances first increase cash and a liability; applying them to new bills reduces both the advance and subsequent collectible AR. The ₹5 crore advance branch needs an explicit scenario gate and does not add cumulative revenue.

Inventory target = next six months' average monthly material demand × inventory days / (365/12). This ties stock investment to the crop/project cycle and future seasonal demand. Base inventory days 150 is a sensitivity assumption, not a verified stock ageing result. Purchases = max(material usage + target stock − opening stock + disease write-off, 0). Half of purchases are paid immediately and half next month; opening payables are paid in month one. Inventory write-offs require replacement cash when stock targets are maintained. Supplier/prepaid balances and operating accruals scale with annual demand. These assumptions can create a large first-month funding requirement because March balances were artificially placed before the winter project season; that is not a reconstructed actual November cash deficit.

No revenue capacity is created before commissioning. Opening CWIP moves into PPE after the same assumed delay but earns no invented additional capacity.

Financing waterfall and failure

Each active month follows opening cash → collections/advances → operating/material/tax outflows → interest/fees → maintenance/growth/event capex → due principal → conditional new debt → conditional equity → closing cash. Interest is charged on beginning debt plus a full month on new drawings, a conservative timing assumption. No retrospective borrowing finances an already-failed month. Monthly netting does not establish intramonth liquidity adequacy.

Base assumed reserve is the greater of ₹2 crore and half a month's normalized cash operating costs. Failure means inability to meet that modeled floor, not necessarily negative cash or a legal default. Both actual cash and reserve shortfall are retained. The model stops economic projection after failure, preserving calendar placeholders, and does not use terminal equity to rescue it.

- Historical cash credit: assumed continued annually in conditional mains, paying 0.5% renewal fee. Strict cases test demand call at month 1 or 12 and no new funding; another strict case keeps existing credit but permits no new sources. Call dates are assumptions because the facility is on demand, not verified fixed maturity.

- Vehicle debt: current maturity spread over twelve months; remainder spread over the following 48 months. Detailed due dates are not known.

- New revolving facility: base conditional limit ₹15 crore outstanding, ₹25 crore cumulative gross draws, 14% annual interest, 1% draw fee, 24-month tranche maturity. New drawings include the cash cost of their own interest/fees in the funding calculation. Every refinance consumes cumulative draw capacity; outstanding and gross-draw limits are separate. No actual sanction is claimed.

- Equity: only when necessary and the scenario gate is enabled, through month 24; price ₹46.875, a 25% discount to the stale ₹62.50 reference, and 2% issue cost. Shares are rounded UP to whole units, and maximum issuance is capped by the 25 million authorization. Gross proceeds, net cash, fees and dilution are distinct. The model cannot assert the placement is executable.

- Expanded search envelope: a separately labeled, unsupported hypothetical ₹60 crore outstanding facility/₹120 crore lifetime draws, authorization to 40 million shares, equity availability to month 60 and conditional ₹4.4245 crore deposit release. It is not silently substituted for base funding.

Unauthorized equity attempts, bank-limit exhaustion, missed renewal, receipt/price stresses and restricted-IPO misuse have explicit test paths. Rejected events do not become cash. A failed assumed branch changes neither G3 evidence nor any live score.

Terminal valuation and investor returns

No dividends are paid, so no retained cash is counted twice. Terminal operating capital = billed AR + unbilled + inventory + supplier/prepaid assets − payables − operating accruals − customer advances + PPE + CWIP. Tax/financial/non-operating assets are excluded consistently.

Terminal NOPAT = positive final-year operating EBIT × (1 − 25% assumed tax) × (1 + terminal growth).

Terminal growth reinvestment = max(terminal invested capital, 0) × terminal growth. This supports growth rather than capitalizing NOPAT as if all were distributable. A further conservative replacement allowance equals max(final maintenance capex − D&A, 0) × (1 + growth).

Terminal FCFF = terminal NOPAT − growth reinvestment − replacement allowance.

Terminal EV = max(terminal FCFF, 0) / (WACC − terminal growth). Negative terminal FCFF is disclosed and capitalized at zero, not converted into a negative perpetual claim on common shareholders. Baseline nominal-INR WACC is 16%, terminal growth 4%, both analyst choices; a 14/16/18/20% WACC × 2/3/4/5% terminal-growth grid is retained. WACC is distinct from the 12/15/18% equity hurdle.

Terminal equity = max(EV + unrestricted cash above next-year operating reserve − debt − ₹0.6044 crore pension/senior claim, 0). All remaining restricted deposits and unspecified advances/non-operating investments are excluded. Customer advances and trade liabilities are already in operating capital and are not deducted a second time.

Terminal price = equity value × 10 million / final whole diluted shares. The investor calculation uses that diluted price, a 1% entry cost and 1% exit cost; a 5% each-way sensitivity is separately retained. Conditional annual return = [terminal price × (1 − exit cost) / (₹62.50 × (1 + entry cost))]^(1/years) − 1. This is not an ordinary/source-eligible IRR and does not establish executable trade prices.

The 8× terminal EBITDA enterprise-value cross-check, and a 6× compression case, use the same terminal cash/debt/claims/shares bridge. It is a cross-check, not a rerating override of FCFF economics. Present-value FCFF/terminal-EV and terminal share are shown as enterprise diagnostics only; a historical-share DCF amount is explicitly not a buyable entry fair value when future equity contributes capital and creates new claims. Hurdle entry prices instead discount funded terminal diluted proceeds at 12/15/18%, net of entry costs.
