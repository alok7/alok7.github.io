4. Business Overview

Business overview

Shri Ahimsa Naturals Limited is a Jaipur-based B2B ingredient processor, not a consumer beverage brand. Incorporated in 1990 and renamed in April 2023, it refines crude caffeine recovered from coffee decaffeination and other natural sources into caffeine anhydrous natural, also produces green coffee bean extract (GCE), and trades/outsources botanical extracts. Food/beverage, nutraceutical and cosmetic manufacturers and resellers are customers. The operating business is at Bagru; wholly owned Shri Ahimsa Healthcare Private Limited (SAHPL) houses the Sawarda expansion. Consolidated accounts therefore capture the economic investment; parent-only profitability overstates group earnings through intercompany interest. BUS01 pp139–143; BUS02 printed2–6,16, note36

The business earns a conversion spread: secure natural feedstock, extract with adequate yield and purity, satisfy customer qualification, sell a consistent ingredient, and collect after funding inventory and credit. This is not royalty income. Natural origin can differentiate the product but does not remove synthetic substitutes, competing extractors or customer bargaining power. Green coffee extract recovers additional value from feedstock; botanical cross-selling uses existing relationships but has not yet proven equivalent in-house economics. 5. Moat and Industry Position

Moat and industry position

The defensibility mechanism is process learning plus customer qualification, not an established monopoly. Management claims 97% extraction efficiency, 99.9% purity and decades of proprietary development. Customer certification and repeat qualification can create switching friction: failure of a low-cost ingredient may jeopardize a much higher-value end product. Recovery of GCE alongside caffeine and backward integration could lower effective input cost. These mechanisms are economically plausible; independent competing-plant yield/cost data, verified global market share, patent exclusivity and contractual pricing power were not located. "Largest in Asia" and "lowest-cost globally" remain management claims. Certification is necessary market access, not automatic pricing power. BUS02 printed1–5; BUS03 PDF5–6

Concentration limits the moat claim. RHP audited-certificate tables show FY2024 largest customer 65.95%, top 5 84.93%, top 10 93.66%; H1 FY2025 these improved to 34.73%, 56.05%, 74.28%. A six-month improvement cannot establish full-cycle diversification. The largest supplier was 73.21% of FY2024 purchases, top 5 95.47%; H1 FY2025 largest 44.56%, top 5 83.18%. Bulk imported feedstock creates interruption/price/working-capital risk even with many end markets. Customers are not necessarily exclusive and may dual-source or integrate. Latest FY2026 customer and supplier concentration were not located. BUS01 pp28–30, customer-risk disclosures

The strongest disconfirming facts are the FY2024 revenue reversal after an exceptional FY2023, substantial unutilized capacity before the new project, concentrated inputs and sales, and lower-cost synthetic alternatives. Historical caffeine-plus-crude production fell 220 MT in FY2023 to 156 MT in FY2024 while installed capacity remained 270 MT. That is a utilization-driven cycle, not proof of inexorable market-share gain. BUS01 printed150

A defensible MOAT 5/10, Medium is supportable: real technical/customer friction but unverified cost leadership and concentration constrain durability. Do not award 7+ simply for age, certifications or natural branding. Peer evidence in the separate comparison shows much larger xanthine producers and a differentiated decaffeination model; no listed exact pure-play was identified. Enterprise value, equity, return and DCF

For funding-complete paths, terminal EV=last 12 month operating EBITDA×exitmultiple. Common equity subtracts debt and unpaid separate claims, adds only available cash above the operating reserve, and divides by terminal issued/diluted claims. Excluded deposits/unused earmarked funds are not silently added again. Dividends (10% annual modeled PAT, constrained by cash reserve) leave bank cash before terminal value. Thus dividends are not counted both in IRR and retained cash. Investor IRR uses monthly dates and 1% entry/exit execution assumptions, before personal taxes.12/15/18% hurdle prices are recomputed from the same cash distributions. These costs are illustrative, not observed order-book impact.

FCFF uses operating collections minus supplier/operating cash, unlevered tax, capex and other operating working-capital movements. Financing cash and investment income are excluded. Reserve investment is deducted. DCF runs through the finite funding transition, not just the sale horizon; terminal NOPAT, D&A, replacement, growth investment, working-capital growth and reserve growth reconcile. Terminal growth 0–4% is always below WACC12–18%; base 15% WACC is an analytical risk range, not mechanically the equity hurdle. Terminal value uses sustainable cash after those reinvestment needs; a negative terminal FCFF gets no positive terminal value. Opening debt/claims are subtracted once and opening available cash less required reserve is added with its signed adjustment (deficit is not clipped away). Financed equity cash is included with new claims. FD release cash is recognized once at its modeled date, never both opening and future cash. Source-safe rows expose each bridge. DCF/share and exit-multiple IRR can disagree; neither is averaged into an invented target.

Expectations and 5×/10×

Growth×exitmultiple, margin×WACC and WACC×terminalgrowth are complete two-way grids, with failures displayed rather than suppressed. Reverse 15% hurdle tests scan 0–100% nominal demand growth then bisect only a funded adjacent crossing; a failure or lack of crossing is retained as an unsuccessful solve, not replaced by an unsupported required CAGR. Capacity saturation and financing can break monotonicity; any bracket is local and conditional, not unique. 11. Bear Case and Permanent Loss Path

The strongest counterargument to the growth thesis is that much of the historical revenue surge was realization-led, while the company is committing far more capital just as operating margins normalize. The primary RHP attributes the FY2023 caffeine sales spike mainly to higher realizations; production then fell 220→156 MT in FY2024. New capacity must earn returns rather than merely enlarge assets. BUS01

A permanent-loss sequence is credible without alleging misconduct: a major customer delays or cuts orders; natural/synthetic price competition compresses contribution; imported inventory and receivables absorb cash; project commissioning/qualification slips; maintenance and committed project payments remain due; and shareholders must accept unfavorable debt/equity financing or lost capacity opportunities. At an equity value over ₹ 1,000 crore, an otherwise surviving business can still cause investment loss through margin/multiple normalization and dilution. The monthly model combines these effects instead of independently shocking P/E alone.

The conservative boundary deliberately withholds unverified FD availability and unspent post-March receipts. Its failures demonstrate dependence on those conditions, not actual insolvency. Conversely, conditional FD access does not prove money remains free in October. The decisive thesis-break variable is cash-funded, customer-qualified commissioning at acceptable incremental returns: inability to reconcile financing and deliver this after further delays would undermine the growth thesis.
