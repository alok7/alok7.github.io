Hold — unresolved, not an evidence-backed compounder. Shivashrit is an operating potato-flake ingredient manufacturer with demonstrated prior capacity and revenue growth, but concentrated uncontracted buyers, capital-intensive seasonal procurement and weak cumulative cash realization limit quality. 4. Business Overview

Business and value chain

Shivashrit Foods Limited (NSE Emerge SHIVASHRIT, ISIN INE1DLF01018) was incorporated on 23 August 2017 and commenced commercial potato-flake production in 2018. The public-company conversion certificate was issued on 4 October 2024. It processes potatoes in Aligarh, Uttar Pradesh into dehydrated flakes and, on customer request, milled flour. Industrial snack and processed-food manufacturers buy consistency, food safety, rehydration and functional texture rather than a consumer-facing story. Washing, peeling, blanching, cooking, mashing and drum drying convert a bulky perishable crop into shelf-stable ingredients. The plant, associated water/energy supply, cold storage and working-capital funding are all necessary parts of the economics. SFL-P, printed pp 137–149

The business is overwhelmingly B2B. FY25 domestic B2B revenue was ₹81.3843 crore and exports ₹23.2922 crore; own/third-party website sales together were only ₹0.0169 crore, about 0.016% of total revenue. The December 2024 consumer launch is therefore an option, not an established brand moat or a reason to apply branded-FMCG valuations. FY26 export FOB revenue fell to ₹21.1847 crore, despite total revenue increasing to ₹113.5147 crore: domestic growth, not broad export acceleration, drove the latest year. The September 2026 AGM describes first steps into snacks, but no material separately profitable consumer franchise is demonstrated. SFL-P p 140; SFL-AR26R note 37; SFL-AGM26 pp 2–3

FY25 export sales were concentrated in Israel (43.97% of exports), Belgium (33.41%) and Bangladesh (18.85%). Export destination count and a list of historical markets do not establish diversified current earnings. Current FY26 country-level concentration and exact customer-level pricing contracts are ND. FY25 Uttar Pradesh generated 40.68% of domestic sales, with Gujarat 20.33% and Maharashtra 17.87%; these are shares of domestic, not total revenue. SFL-P pp 140–141

5. Moat and Industry Position

Concentration, procurement and moat mechanism

FY25 top-five customers represented 79.10% and top-ten 88.39% of revenue. The prospectus explicitly says there were no legally binding/formal customer agreements, while describing repeat relationships and no historical major-customer loss. Both observations matter: repeat orders support commercial competence, but do not create contracted backlog or lock-in. Top-ten concentration increased from 73.33% in FY24. Customer identities, current FY26 concentration and the margin effect of losing the largest customer remain ND. Older FY23 percentage and rupee concentration tables do not exactly reconcile to reported revenue; do not silently adopt their percentages. SFL-P pp 27–28

The procurement narrative emphasizes farmers, but FY25 source mix was 9.32% farmers,88.60% traders and 2.08% other suppliers. There were 114 associated farmers, not 114 contracted exclusive suppliers. No long-term purchase arrangements were disclosed. Top-five suppliers supplied 38.61% and top-ten 60.41% of FY25 purchases. Historical supplier tables use inconsistent potato-only versus total-purchase denominators; the clearly reconciled FY25 figures are the comparison anchor, not evidence of a perfectly integrated seed-to-shelf system. SFL-P pp 28–29,149–150

Potential advantages should be interpreted mechanism-by-mechanism:

- Procurement proximity and adjacent storage reduce logistics/spoilage risk. Evidence is the Aligarh location and leased cold storage. Durability is moderate because other UP processors, including Agristo Masa, also access this crop belt. Failure modes are bad processing-potato crops, traders repricing supply and cold-storage losses.

- Process control and customer qualification can create repeat-purchase friction. The prospectus describes automated imported Tummers equipment and food-safety certifications. These are capabilities and entry costs, not patented exclusivity. A funded entrant can buy comparable equipment and seek qualification; exact qualification time and loss rates are ND.

- A consistent low-moisture ingredient can command better economics than raw potatoes, but pass-through/pricing power is not demonstrated. Large concentrated buyers and international alternative suppliers constrain price; input-cost and output-price cycles can move differently.

- Brand/network moat is unproven. Retail revenue was immaterial, patents are not the thesis, trademark applications were pending and copyright applications objected at the prospectus date. These IP application statuses are historical disclosure, not proof of infringement or current rejection. SFL-P pp 137–159

A narrow-to-moderate processing capability is defensible; an enduring high-moat platform is not established. The proposition is an agricultural-ingredient capacity expansion with capital/working-capital discipline, not a demonstrated asset-light compounder.

Denominator and comparability safeguards

1. Iscon operating margin and Agristo OPBDIT are agency-defined operating measures, not automatically identical to issuer model EBITDA. Report each definition and avoid basis-point precision across them.

2. GCA days, receivable days and net working-capital days have different denominators and components. They cannot be ranked as if each were CCC. Agriculture requires seasonal monthly funding analysis even when annual working capital falls.

3. Comparable audited CFO and consistent operating invested capital were not established for these peers. CFO conversion and ROIC are therefore ND, not imputed from EBITDA, PAT, cash accrual or sponsor support. No peer ROIC premium is asserted.

4. Standalone and consolidated scopes, FY26 estimates, FY25 audited figures and CY24 audited figures are explicitly separated. No future expansion capacity is treated as delivered production.

5. Shivashrit’s proposed expansion adds process capacity but also fixed assets, start-up cost and procurement finance. The relevant benchmark question is sustainable margin after utilization, interest, tax and recurring capital/working-capital needs, not whether a global potato market is large.

Investment implication

The peer evidence supports a viable but capital- and crop-sensitive processing industry. It does not prove a durable high-return moat for Shivashrit. Larger peers demonstrate both achievable margins and substantial financing needs; a credible sponsor does not eliminate ramp-up losses. For Shivashrit, customer concentration, site execution, actual seasonal funding and related-party economics are more decision-useful than importing a private peer’s credit rating or mature overseas margin into a valuation multiple.

7. Growth Quality and Reinvestment Runway

Industry position and accessible runway

Shelf-stable potato ingredients benefit from convenience-food demand, industrial snacks, product consistency and export access. Food safety, usable potato varieties, drying technology and working capital create nontrivial entry requirements. However, the relevant competitive set includes larger processors and international suppliers, not only similarly sized listed SME food companies. Iscon Balaji, Asandas/Hy Fun and Agristo Masa provide primary-source evidence of both viable scale and the risk of debt-funded ramp-up. Agristo NV is a separate mature international benchmark, not a fourth independent Indian peer or a second unrelated group. See the peer comparison for period and scope limitations.

The issuer's prospectus cites Department of Commerce potato-flake export values of US$58.09 m in FY24 and US$63.27 m in FY25 (HS11052000). This is an export-flow statistic, not the whole Indian market, and the prospectus's broad potato or frozen-potato tables use other HS codes. It cannot justify a market-share percentage for flakes or prove that planned output can be sold. No independently verified current domestic market share has been established. A credible bottom-up runway is utilization of existing equipment, commissioning the additional line, customer onboarding, collections and a reasonable share of relevant industrial demand. SFL-P pp 134–136

The physical expansion arithmetic is attractive only if customers, crop procurement, storage, energy, permits and cash grow together. Existing 8,640-tonne annual capacity plus the proposed 15,840-tonne line imply 24,480 tonnes at the prospectus's 300 working days. The larger separate site changes logistics from the original adjacent-site plan. No capacity percentage is automatically translated into revenue: volume is constrained by available capacity, ramp, customer uptake and procurement funding in the model. Management's January 2027 target is an assumption to test, not a commissioning certificate. SFL-P; SFL-EGM26; SFL-AGM26

Growth quality is not the same as fast historical sales. Raw GQS is 6.35, but persistent weak CASH=3 activates the framework's cap of 5. Shivashrit Foods monthly valuation and funding model

What the model establishes

This is a conditional, source-anchored operating and funding experiment, not a current bank reconciliation or a forecast that the company will default. It starts its hypothetical monthly clock in November 2026, with 60- and 120-month holding periods at the observed 19 October 2026 price of ₹114.80. All money is in ₹ crore, except per-share prices and explicit rupee inputs. Physical production is in metric tonnes. Current issued shares are 18,269,000.

A numerical conditional return is not an ordinary IAS base-case return. CARE could not independently ascertain the expansion attribution of ₹17.06 crore working-capital use at June 2026. The complete March-to-current financial bridge, actual remaining project-loan drawing power, supplier advance attribution and premature redemption terms for IPO deposits are also unverified. These are specific unresolved funding/use-of-proceeds inputs, not a general rule that old financial statements make every SME unscoreable. All paths retain source_eligible=false until those material points are resolved. This does not by itself establish G1 misconduct or an actual G3 default.

Dated opening state and bridge

The model does not silently mix a June cash balance with a March balance sheet. The starting construction is explicit and hypothetical:

1. Audited March cash of ₹27.4089 crore includes ₹23.6521 crore of unused IPO proceeds. The mathematical general-cash residual is ₹3.7568 crore, not ₹27.4089 crore plus IPO cash. It is not independently confirmed usable October liquidity.

2. March IPO capex, working-capital and general-corporate pools are ₹18.1512 crore, ₹5.0945 crore and ₹0.4064 crore. June monitoring reports ₹16.49 crore, ₹1.94 crore and ₹0.36 crore. Their ₹18.79 crore sum is ₹0.01 crore below the rounded total of ₹18.80 crore. The difference is excluded, not manufactured as liquidity.

3. Only the identified capex object reduction of ₹1.6612 crore is added to the March committed-project proxy and deducted from remaining budget. The ₹3.1545 crore working-capital reduction is added to supplier advances as a management-consistent modeling hypothesis, not verified expansion deployment. Other intervening cash, asset, liability and income movements are unknown. Holding them unchanged is a scenario assumption, not a claim about June or October accounts.

4. March CWIP of ₹19.7502 crore and capital advances of ₹8.0275 crore are separate components of a ₹27.7777 crore committed-project proxy. They are counted once. Against the ₹61.30 crore source project budget, the post-bridge remaining project amount is ₹31.8611 crore. Existing equipment PPE of ₹15.4838 crore is separate. Capital advances are not also left in operating working capital.

5. Supplier advances, GST input, prepaid expense, duty receivable and operating security deposits are retained in working capital with separate purposes. Capital advances, the pledged FD, tax-refund receivable and nonoperating assets are not misclassified as freely available operating cash.

The proxy does not establish that all CWIP or all advances belong exclusively to the new plant, that ₹61.30 crore is an all-in final cost, or that no other expenditure occurred after March. Those unresolved mappings are reasons for conditional interpretation. Overrun, timing, GST and FX stresses are retained.

IPO and deposit cash controls

Capex IPO money can pay only modeled project expenditure. Working-capital IPO money can reimburse only incremental operating assets net of trade financing. It cannot fund old debt repayments, taxes, general operating losses or dividends merely because cash is needed. The general-corporate remainder is excluded from general liquidity rather than treated as unrestricted debt-service money. No OFS proceeds are added to company cash.

The June deployment schedule contains ₹15.52 crore of deposits. The maturity ladder is ₹2 crore in March 2027, ₹2.5 crore in September 2027, ₹10.02 crore in October 2027 and ₹1 crore in December 2027. The source-bounded stress allocates the full locked deposit balance to the capex pool, a conservative but explicitly unverified allocation because object-by-deposit mapping is absent. It releases principal only at those maturities. The conditional cases assume permitted premature encashment; separate 1% and 3% redemption-cost sensitivities test the cost. Interest income on these deposits is not assumed to fund the project. Neither deposit ownership nor a future maturity proves immediate unrestricted cash access.

The ₹0.1083 crore pledged SBI FD remains outside usable cash unless an explicit release condition is selected. Its value is not added to terminal equity while its associated restriction remains.

Project debt and existing obligations

March debt is ₹47.5211 crore: ₹18.2983 crore of cash/packing credit and ₹29.2228 crore of term debt, including ₹3.6534 crore of current term maturities. Existing disclosed lending rates are approximately 8.75%, with a small car-loan component at 8.7%. The model uses 8.75% for the aggregated existing and project facilities, while testing higher rates. The aggregation is an approximation, not an instrument-perfect bank schedule.

The source reports a ₹35 crore project sanction, but does not separate current old and new HDFC balances sufficiently to verify undrawn availability. Assuming no intervening draws and a still-effective sanction, subtracting all ₹25.7194 crore current HDFC term balance gives a conservative arithmetic headroom of ₹9.2806 crore. Keeping up to the prior ₹11.3147 crore old HDFC balance gives an upper arithmetic headroom of ₹20.5953 crore. Neither bound is a bank-confirmed facility. The conditional reference uses ₹15.3711 crore, derived from project committed-capital minus capex IPO use as an illustrative debt-allocation proxy; it is not a disclosed loan balance. Both source-bound alternatives are tested.

The first twelve months repay the disclosed current term maturity evenly. Subsequent aggregated old-term amortization closes the modeled balance by month 48. Source term tenures span 48–96 months; this is an accelerated stress schedule, not asserted actual contractual dates. Newly drawn project debt starts amortization at month 6 over a 72-month assumption, with a final payoff at month 78. Interest is charged on opening debt and on new draws in the month drawn. Existing credit renewal is allowed in the reference cases, consistent with annual-renewal facilities; an on-demand call/no-renewal case is a separate stress, not silently imposed as the ordinary base.

Additional flexible borrowing is wholly hypothetical. Reference cases cap outstanding extra debt at ₹80 crore and gross cumulative draws at ₹160 crore, priced at 13% with fees and 36-month maturities. The target grid permits larger stated caps, not evidence of lender consent. Draws solve the current-month fee, interest and tax interaction, rather than filling the cash gap and charging interest only later. Renewal fees and repayments are explicit. No new flexible debt, equity rescue or flexible-debt renewal is allowed after the holding-period exit; the terminal cash test continues long enough to include its maturities. Merely moving a loan beyond year five cannot make it disappear.

Production, growth and procurement

The verified existing capacity is 8,640 tonnes per year. The proposed plant adds 15,840 tonnes, producing aggregate capacity of 24,480 tonnes. This is supported by the final prospectus and the November 2025 presentation, not inferred from a generic sector expansion. The January 2027 commissioning date is September 2026 management guidance after earlier June/November dates; it is not observed commissioning.

The model permits productive capacity only after the remaining modeled project budget is paid and the chosen commissioning date is reached. A subsequent utilization ramp is explicit. Later expansion purchases have their own 18-month construction lead time. Pending initial-project capacity is included in committed capacity when deciding whether another order is needed, avoiding redundant plant orders. Initial-project commissioning does not prematurely capitalize unrelated future expansion assets.

FY25 actual production is 7,019 tonnes and revenue ₹104.6934 crore. FY26 physical output and realized price were not verified. The FY26 unit base is therefore 7610.4098 constant-FY25-realization equivalent tonnes, calculated from FY26 revenue ₹113.5147 crore. It is not claimed actual production or a proven organic-volume bridge. Annual demand growth and price growth are separate assumptions. Each modeled year's demand is constrained by productive capacity; unmet demand is not booked as revenue.

Reference variable input cost is the FY26 materials, purchases and inventory-change proxy of ₹80.6109 crore divided by ₹113.5147 crore sales. This includes more than raw potatoes, so the input-price shock is a broad purchased-input stress, not an exact commodity-price forecast. The EBITDA-margin parameter is a current-scale reference margin, not a flat realized margin every year. Thirty percent of other operating costs is held fixed against original sales, with the balance varying with output. Annual tables therefore show the actual modeled operating leverage and realized EBITDA.

The source says approximately 80–90% of potatoes are procured in December–March. Seasonal monthly inventory-cover assumptions produce a high March supply buffer and a lower pre-harvest buffer. They are not claims of exact monthly historical stocks. Supplier advances can secure part of that supply, so the model does not demand two complete buffers of potatoes, one in inventory and another in advances. The supply target subtracts the explicitly assumed usable advance backing, subject to a minimum inventory floor. Separate cases remove or halve that usability.

Advances are applied to purchases before the remaining invoices become payables. New cash advances replenish only the stated target; they are not magically refunded as cash. A 50% target-normalization case, full application/release case, stuck-advance case and 150% target case retain different financing consequences. Beginning receivables and payables settle through separate queues. Delayed collections, loss allowances, spoilage, supplier terms, raw-price shocks and demand loss affect cash as well as earnings. The treatment distinguishes a plausible procurement explanation from independently verified expansion use.

Taxes, capital costs and exceptional claims

The main normalized tax assumption is 29.12%, the rate disclosed in the source tax computation. It assumes no indefinite continuation of current 80 IB relief; the exact fiscal expiry was not verified and is not invented. The current-tax benefit sensitivity is explicit. Tax is not credited on losses unless cash realization is demonstrated. Original tax provisions are settled separately from new tax expense, and normal depreciation is not assumed identical to maintenance capex.

Capex GST/import duties are not certified included in the ₹61.30 crore budget. The base does not insert an invented tax payable; the 18% cash-tax stresses show delayed full recovery and no recovery. The FX stress applies a disclosed imported-cost-share approximation to remaining project costs, not a claim that every open invoice remains in euros. Maintenance spending is at least the scenario sales ratio; terminal replacement is no less than modeled depreciation. Growth reinvestment is the greater of explicit capacity plus working-capital needs and an economic NOPAT× growth/ROIC floor.

The source's old tax demand was resolved and the disputed bank fee was already debited; neither is charged again as a new cash outflow. The remaining ₹0.07 crore guarantee is tested separately. Larger legal-payment cases are hypothetical severity tests, not allegations that such debts exist. Employee claims of ₹0.3503 crore remain explicit senior claims. Nonoperating mutual funds, property advances, gold, the related advance and tax-refund receivable total ₹4.8022 crore before recovery assumptions. Conditional exit recovery is tested at 0%, 50% and 100% of source carrying amount. Book value is not asserted market value, and the same asset is not also counted as opening cash.

Terminal valuation and failure gates

After the 5- or 10-year holding period, the model runs at least twelve months and long enough to test outstanding flexible/project maturities. The first twelve months determine recurring terminal FCFF. Later transition months test survival, cash reserves and repayments. Replacement and growth cash requirements continue through the transition, scaled with terminal growth. Capacity shortfalls prevent capitalizing an undeliverable terminal demand path.

Terminal enterprise value is recurring FCFF divided by WACC less terminal growth. The main WACC is 16%, separately sensitized at 13–20%; terminal growth is tested at 2–5%, always below WACC. The 15% equity hurdle is not used as WACC. An EBITDA-multiple cross-check includes a compression case.

The enterprise-to-common-equity bridge subtracts debt and remaining senior claims, adds only cash above operating requirements and the separately qualified nonoperating recovery, and divides by actual scenario shares. Debt-service earmarked cash is not deducted twice: operating and financing reserves are shown separately, debt is deducted once, and a separate funding-reserve test must pass. No dividend is paid and also retained in exit cash. Cashflows use nominal INR; personal investor taxes are excluded. Entry and exit execution costs are 1% and 2%, with wider-cost sensitivities.

A path failing its liquidity floor, funding cap or terminal replacement/capacity/reserve condition retains all rows up to failure and an explicit reason. It has no ordinary or conditional terminal return. A liquidity-floor shortfall is not an observed corporate default; the liquidity floor itself is an analyst choice. Funded conditional paths retain numeric price/return diagnostics, but ordinary IAS returns stay NS until the identified source gaps are resolved.

Hypothetical new shares are issued as whole shares at a stated price and cost; cash and dilution enter together. The largest test remains below the source authorized-capital ceiling. No future issuance approval or investor funding is represented as obtained.

Sensitivities, reverse expectations and outcome grid

The complete dataset preserves every tested growth/margin pair, WACC/terminal-growth pair, financing, timing, advance, cash, fee, tax and dilution path. Hurdle entry prices are computed at 12%, 15% and 18%, but are conditional outputs rather than buy limits. Reverse expectations can be read from the funded grid: no supported growth forecast is invented to fill a missing crossing. A finite grid with no crossing does not prove a target is impossible.

The 5× and 10× tests use five- and ten-year horizons, actual modeled shares and financing, execution costs, capacity and terminal cash. A tenfold stock price needs approximately 58.49% annually over five years or 25.89% over ten years before implementation costs. All source-eligible crossing counts remain zero; none is sold as a proven multibagger.

Reproducibility and model limitations

The current canonical gzip includes inputs, all path assumptions, monthly rows, annual rows, terminal attempts, funded values and failed outcomes. Decimal arithmetic and deterministic gzip timestamps make exact rebuilds possible. Independent arithmetic checks do not import the production engine. In-memory mutation controls verify that key cash, asset, debt, interest, share and terminal-price discrepancies are caught.

Earlier developmental model versions are preserved with their source inputs, runtime and expected canonical hashes. They are labeled superseded, including errors found during construction, and are not alternative approved research. Their outputs can be rebuilt in a fresh directory without modifying the saved originals. Model assurance demonstrates reproducibility and internal arithmetic, not that assumptions will occur or that unavailable primary information has become known.

11. Bear Case and Permanent Loss Path

The strongest permanent-loss path is not simply a lower potato price or a temporarily weak quote. It is a linked sequence: the larger plant is delayed or ramps slowly; concentrated industrial customers do not absorb output at expected processing margins; seasonal potatoes and supplier advances consume cash; collections lengthen; restricted IPO deposits cannot cover unrelated losses or debt; lender renewal or additional capital becomes necessary. If funding is supplied on unattractive terms, aggregate profit growth can coexist with weak per-share value. If the new machinery becomes economically redundant or advances do not convert into usable supply, accounting book value is not equivalent to cash recovery.

The critical thesis variable is cash earned per additional rupee committed to mature processing capacity. The strongest evidence against a favorable compounder interpretation is weak multi-year operating realization despite a profitable existing business, plus the monitor's specific deployment-verification gap before the new unit operates. Conversely, the earlier line was expanded successfully, the main audit and IFC opinion are clean, source stock/bank-return checks did not find material discrepancies, and an old tax dispute was resolved. Those facts limit the claim: the evidence supports investigation and conditional modeling, not a finding of fraud or observed insolvency. SFL-AR26R; SFL-MONJUN26

Permanent impairment risk is assessed High on this unresolved expansion case because procurement, capex, timing and financing can fail together. This is an analyst risk classification, not a default probability. A modeled reserve shortfall is neither a bank covenant violation nor an observed company default. A failed model path has no ordinary terminal target or IRR; recovery would depend on actual asset saleability, customer economics, senior claims and financing terms that are not certified by the model. Clearing the deployment and funding evidence, commissioning on revised terms and sustained mature-line cash conversion would change the thesis more than one favorable trading day.
