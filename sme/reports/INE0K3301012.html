Reconsider after verified funding, subsidy collection and full-cycle unit returns improve.

4. Business Overview

Business and economic engine

Shreedhar Spinners manufactures compact-spun cotton yarn, predominantly Ne 10s–40s, at one Amravati MIDC Textile Park site. Customers are manufacturers, fabric processors, exporters and traders; end uses include knitting, weaving, apparel and home textiles. It is not a branded garment business and does not own the customer's end-market demand. Production began in November 2022. Shreedhar Cotsyn is the holding company and an export distribution channel; the issuer itself had no subsidiary, associate or JV at the prospectus date. Standalone accounts therefore represent the investable operating entity. B01 pp 136–143,164

The money-making mechanism is the spread between yarn realizations and cotton, power, labor and financing costs, with substantial machinery and cotton inventory required before collection. Compact-spinning equipment, contamination sorting and laboratory testing support consistent specifications, but similar machinery is purchasable by competitors. Cotton proximity in Vidarbha and the parent's distribution experience are real operating advantages, not evidence of protected pricing power. Product sale proceeds must be separated from government incentive accruals in operating revenue. B01 pp 136–143,213,216; B02

FY26 goods sales were ₹138.6119 crore; GST subsidy ₹3.1344 crore, interest subsidy ₹5.0189 crore and negative FX ₹0.3942 crore brought reported operating revenue to ₹146.3710 crore. Thus 5.57% of reported revenue was gross GST/interest subsidy. Power subsidies and electricity-duty benefits also support costs and should not be assumed captured by simply deleting those two revenue lines. This is legitimate disclosed incentive income, not alleged fabricated sales. The investment question is its duration, collection and effect on unsubsidized returns. B01 pp 213,216

The evidence supports MOAT 4, not a structural compounder premium. Useful customer relationships, modern technology and location coexist with commodity competition, purchase-order sales and limited ability to pass through cotton inflation immediately. FY26 revenue rises 9.02% and goods sales 8.85%, while the subsidy/FX mix contributes to reported growth. Goods revenue grows ₹11.2674 crore; operating subsidies grow ₹1.6174 crore, but FX deteriorates ₹0.7804 crore. 7. Growth Quality and Reinvestment Runway

Accessible runway, projects and funding

DUR 5: existing capacity and distribution provide tangible addressable headroom, but customer orders, funding and commodity economics constrain the opportunity. The 10,000 MT nameplate at an unchanged FY26 goods-sales/output ratio implies roughly ₹230.52 crore of goods revenue at 100% utilization, or ₹219.00 crore at 95%. These are physical illustrations, not forecasts; count mix, waste, inventory and selling prices can change. At 12,000 MT the same mechanical 100% capacity value is ₹276.63 crore. Neither illustrates multi-billion global TAM conversion or assured demand. B01; B03

The January 2026 commissioned Unit II cost ₹58.18 crore according to BWR and was in stabilization. The August 4, 2026 separate board approval proposes ₹45 crore of additional machinery/infrastructure, raising 10,000→12,000 MT by Q2 FY2027–28 through internal accruals and debt. The filing calls this completion of the second unit and states approximately 95% utilization, without disclosing a dated actual-output bridge supporting a full 10,000 MT annual run rate. Do not conflate the prospectus's weighted FY26 utilization with current full-capacity utilization. Also do not pay the old ₹58.18 crore project again in the model. B02 pp 2–4; B03 pp 1–2

The new 45 crore project represents ₹22.5 crore per 1,000 MT additional capacity, about ₹0.976 incremental capex per ₹1 annual goods-sales capacity using the crude FY26 ratio. This is capital intensive, before working capital. Remaining commitments, spend-to-date and sanctioned debt are not established by the announcement. The new promoter-warrant civil allocation may overlap the project: treat overlap explicitly rather than adding ₹3.68017 crore twice without evidence. Restricted IPO proceeds, actual debt repayment, bank collateral and subsidy collection must be bridged separately by the financial model.

Capital returns and reinvestment economics

The reproducible capital proxy is equity plus all interest-bearing debt minus bank cash; it retains debt-service reserves, working capital, subsidy receivables and committed CWIP/capex. It rises from ₹78.9062 crore FY24 to ₹85.5292 crore FY25 and ₹145.5735 crore FY26. At an explicit 26% normalized tax, economic EBIT-based FY26 average ROIC is 8.72%, while excluding operating subsidies/FX yields 3.75%. FY24–26 incremental NOPAT/incremental capital is 3.90% subsidy-inclusive and 0.77% core; the FY25–26 measures are 4.78% and 3.75%. These are diagnostic committed-capital proxies, not precise project IRRs. Both lie below an analytical 11–15% nominal cost-of-capital range. F01 pp197–219; [financial_rebuild.py]

Unit II began production January 15, 2026, so FY26 includes most of its balance-sheet cost but only a partial revenue contribution; that is a legitimate favorable timing explanation. Excluding the new plant from capital while extrapolating its future sales would overstate proven returns. The ₹45 crore later expansion, IPO machinery plan and claimed increased utilization require incremental volume, realization, cotton spread, full depreciation, financing and collection evidence. CAP 3–4 is supportable depending on treatment of incomplete new-plant seasoning: 3 reflects persistently sub-cost observed capital productivity, while 4 gives explicit credit for documented ramp-up timing. Do not call the new unit permanently value-destructive before a full operating cohort, or automatically upgrade CAP on capacity alone.

Governance, ownership and stewardship

B_SHP pp 1–8; B01 p 23

The September 12 proposal, October 3 corrigendum and October 9 voting results authorize 900,900 promoter/group warrants at ₹113 each, maximum ₹10.18017 crore, 25% upfront and 75% on exercise within 18 months. Full conversion would create 22,338,900 shares, a 4.20% increase in claims, and about 71.67% promoter/group ownership. Allocation is ₹3.68017 crore civil works, ₹4 crore unsecured-loan repayment (including possible promoters/directors/related parties), ₹2.5 crore GCP, with use within 12 months of receipt. The estimate is management's, not independently appraised. Warrants do not justify putting ₹10.18 crore cash into a current balance sheet. NSE SME announcements searched September 1–October 20 show approval documents, not an allotment/receipt confirmation. B04; B05; B06; B08

The corrigendum gives statutory floor ₹112.47 versus issue ₹113, relevant date September 9, and notes price recomputation after the required listing period and lock-in until any additional amount is paid. It adds independent-valuer disclosure after the original notice. The regulatory floor is not an analyst fair value, and a discount to an October share price is not by itself evidence of unlawful pricing. Minority economic fairness still deserves scrutiny because the warrants give promoters optionality and may finance repayment to insiders. Scrutinizer reported 690,000 valid votes from 10 voters, all in favor. Do not infer broad minority endorsement from unanimous votes cast or imply promoter votes were the deciding majority. B04–B06

Governance concerns also include multiple late ROC filings (including PAS-6 delays 184/143 days and MGT-14 delay 236 days) and past EPF/GST payment/return delays. Prospectus says records were updated and dues regularized with interest/penalty; it disclosed no proceedings initiated for those ROC lapses as of the prospectus. This is recurring compliance weakness with remedial counterevidence, not a proven fraud allegation. Disclosed tax proceedings against the company total ₹12.64 lakh. A senior manager's road-accident FIR is distinct from corporate financial misconduct; group tax/civil disputes must not be mechanically charged as issuer debt. B01 pp 28–29,37–41,254ff

The restated accounts say the FY26 audit trail operated at application/database levels and was preserved. The later annual audit/control findings should be read with the financial section rather than assumed identical to every earlier statement. Parent distribution support, declining related-party sales and disclosed financing terms are positive counterevidence; family involvement and a clean opinion alone cannot establish exceptional stewardship. Resolve actual project funding, warrant allotment/recomputation and related-party repayment beneficiaries before advancing the investment conclusion.

Incentive duration and normalization: keep support and expiry distinct

The annual report's company overview says the company remains eligible for IPS, capital-in-lieu-of-interest and power benefits; deleting all future support is therefore a stress, not an established outcome. The prospectus's issuer-specific disclosure establishes a seven-year electricity-duty exemption from commencement of production: November 2022 implies approximately November 2029 for the original operation, but a separate Unit II eligibility certificate was not recovered. IPS is tied to gross SGST on eligible first sales billed and delivered in Maharashtra, not all revenue or exports. The exact issuer IPS rupee ceiling and expiry remain unavailable. B01 p 213; [annual report PDF pp 16–17,50]

There is an important finite-subsidy clue. The original February 15, 2018 textile policy pays eligible capital subsidy in installments of 30%, 30% and 40% after 12, 24 and 36 months. Shreedhar's FY24/FY25/FY26 interest-subsidy accruals of ₹3.7641/3.7641/5.0189 crore total ₹12.5471 crore and match that split almost exactly. This is an analyst inference, not a recovered sanction certificate: the original project's capital-linked subsidy may be substantially accrued already, so the FY26 ₹5.0189 crore should not be capitalized as perpetual operating support. New machinery may qualify for additional support only under its own applicable conditions; do not invent a grant. B10 §7.4, pp 10–11; B01 p 216

A separate future-SGST support sensitivity can retain the observed FY26 GST-subsidy/goods-sales ratio of about 2.2613%, with explicitly assumed collection lags and finite durations, while recovering the opening subsidy receivable separately. The general PSI 2019 rules say an eligibility certificate identifies the incentive quantum and period; Mega-project duration is customized by the Cabinet Sub-Committee or High Power Committee. Generic policy duration cannot fill the missing issuer certificate. Power benefits already embedded in operating costs must not be added again as new income, nor described as removed by an ex-GST/interest margin. B11 §§2.7–2.8

11. Bear Case and Permanent Loss Path

The strongest permanent-loss path is a narrow yarn–cotton spread meeting a large capital bill, not necessarily a collapse in reported turnover. Inventory and subsidy receivables can grow while accounting earnings remain positive. The next ₹45 crore expansion then competes with bank amortization, collateral and working capital; delayed incentive payments or parent/customer stress can force new debt or dilutive equity. Pledged promoter shares add potential control risk without establishing an invocation. The thesis-break variable is cash-supported return on the total committed capital, after financing and dilution—not installed spindles alone.

Counterevidence is important: the plant was commissioned, physical output rose, parent sales dependence fell sharply, three-year reported CFO exceeds PAT, and BWR plus the statutory auditor provide favorable liquidity/no-default evidence. The report does not convert incomplete current funding data or conditional laboratory failures into a finding of insolvency, fraud, or disappearing IPO money. The approved warrant repayment to unsecured lenders requires specific lender-consent and use-of-funds evidence, not an unsupported allegation.
